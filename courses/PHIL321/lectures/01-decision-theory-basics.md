## Clarifications

- **Dominance:** act A *weakly* dominates B if A does at least as well as B in every state and strictly better in at least one; *strongly* dominates if strictly better in every state. The "exception" line: dominance is only a valid rule when the states are independent of the act you pick (otherwise e.g. "don't buy insurance" dominates in every state, yet is wrong because the act changes the probabilities).
- **Three decision situations:** certainty (each act → one known outcome), risk (probabilities of states are known), ignorance/uncertainty (no probabilities known). Peterson ch. 2 §2.2 is skipped.
- **Insufficient reason (Laplace):** under ignorance, treat all states as equally probable and maximise expected value.
- **Ordinal vs expected value:** ordinal utilities only rank outcomes; the numbers assigned aren't unique, so two ordinal assignments that agree on the ranking can give different expected values. Expected-value reasoning needs an *interval* (cardinal) scale. That's the point behind the last bullet, not outliers.
