## Look-alikes

Topics that are easy to mix up. When a quiz picks a question from one, a question from its look-alike comes right after it.

| Topic | Look-alike | Why they get confused |
|---|---|---|
| Hindu philosophy and bhakti (Upanishads to Krishna) | Awakening, teaching and death (deeds 10–12) | Hindu thought holds a permanent self, the atman, while the Buddha's awakening finds no such self, anatman, only the five skandhas passing on like fire, so the two doctrines get swapped. |
| Shramanas against the Vedas: Buddha, Mahavira and asceticism | Awakening, teaching and death (deeds 10–12) | Both lectures retell Krishna's two arguments to Arjuna, where dharma means caste duty, but once the Buddha teaches, dharma means his truth and dharmas in the plural are the parts of existence. |
| Renunciation and the path to awakening (deeds 7–9) | Awakening, teaching and death (deeds 10–12) | The meditative absorptions run through both lectures, the teachers' four and then eight before the awakening and the fourth in Harvey or the eighth in the deck at it, so the numbers get attached to the wrong events. |
| Vedic religion: gods, sacrifice, karma and the castes | Hindu philosophy and bhakti (Upanishads to Krishna) | Polytheism, pantheism and panentheism describe the Vedic hymns while henotheism describes Krishna devotion, and the four names sound alike. |
| Renunciation and the path to awakening (deeds 7–9) | Hindu philosophy and bhakti (Upanishads to Krishna) | Brahma is a god, the creator in the Trimurti and a class of gods in the form realm, Brahman is the absolute, and a Brahmin is a priest, and the three names sound alike. |
| The Buddha's teaching: three lakshanas, karma, the six realms and the Four Noble Truths | Buddhism as a world religion and the early schools (arhat vs bodhisattva) | Both lectures teach the three poisons and set the arhat beside the bodhisattva, but lecture 1 names them as the split between the early schools while lecture 5 ties the poisons to the pig, chicken and snake and to the realm each causes, so the animals and the school labels get crossed.
| Early canons and the Buddha's biography: Pali Canon, 29 Buddhas, ten virtues and Buddhist cosmology | Buddhist sacred history: Buddha numbers, Jatakas and the twelve great deeds | Both carry the Buddha numbers (Dipankara 4, Gautama 28, Maitreya 29), Sumedha's vow and the Vessantara Jataka, and lecture 5 adds the asamkhyeya and kalpa figures and the ten virtues in order, so the numbers from one lecture get attached to the other's story.
