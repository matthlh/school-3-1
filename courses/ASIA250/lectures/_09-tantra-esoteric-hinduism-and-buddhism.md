## What tantra means

### The word (slides 2 and 3)
- The term comes from the Sanskrit root *tan-*, "to extend, stretch, expand", so tantra can mean "unfolding", "unraveling" or "continuity".
- It already appears in the Vedic texts (c. 5th century BCE) in the sense of a "ritual framework" or "interweaving of rites".
- It is also used for an "expansion of knowledge", a text woven from threads, a "system of thought" or a "compendium".
- In this course, tantra denotes **esoteric (secret) teachings**.
- The deck's metaphor: tantra is "a continuous thread", the thread being the individual person.
  - The entangled thread is the person caught in samsara, the cycle of births, suffering and death.
  - Untying the knots with the person's own innate abilities is liberation, "like a snake that uncoils itself by itself".

### Exoteric versus esoteric (slides 4, 5 and 7)
- **Exoteric** (common) teachings need no secret initiation, can be followed by anyone, read more or less literally, and in Buddhism are the **sutras**.
- **Esoteric** (secret) teachings need a secret initiation, can be followed only with a master's permission, are written obscurely to encode secret practices, and in Buddhism are the **tantras**.
- In exoteric teachings the deity and the worshipper are distinct (duality).
- In esoteric teachings the worshipper is initiated to view herself as a manifestation of the deity, so the line blurs, and in some tantric traditions it disappears completely (non-duality).
- In exoteric Buddhism such as Theravada, the practitioner acquires awakened qualities (detachment, abandoning anger, direct knowledge of impermanence) one step at a time, over many lifetimes.
- In esoteric Buddhism, the practitioner visualizes herself as a deity that already has every awakened quality. It is instantaneous, but needs initiation from a qualified master, who is also seen as a fully awakened Buddha.

### The "cake painting" objection and the reply (slides 8 and 9)
- The objection: imagining you are free of desire does not free you from desire, like Zen's joke about "eating a cake painting" to satisfy hunger.
- The tantric reply: a cake is not inside you, but awakened qualities already are. According to tantra, every awakened quality is already present in the mind.
- Tantric teachings are "the finger pointing to the moon": pointing does not create a new moon, and visualizations and rituals do not create an awakened mind, they make the person aware of qualities already there.

## Transgression as the path

### Shock methods (slides 10 to 15)
- Zen (Mahayana) agrees with the idea: it uses paradox to shock the mind open to emptiness. The deck's example is the instructor's 2013 koan practice in Korea, where the master demanded "why doesn't Bodhidharma have a beard?" and hit the students who mumbled answers.
- Each tantric method breaks a norm, and the deck names the norm each one breaks.
  - The master asks "what is my next thought going to be?", then shouts or hits the student in the gap between two thoughts. This breaks the precept of **non-violence** (Zen has such methods too).
  - Running naked and shouting downhill through a forest until a collision or exhaustion breaks **propriety**. The deck compares it to the "Ozaki 8" challenges in *Point Break* (2015), whose gang leader is named Bodhi ("Awakening").
  - Covering oneself with the ashes of the dead at a cremation ground and begging with a skull bowl, eating whatever is offered, breaks **purity**, the rule a traditional brahmin keeps. This is the **kapalika** sect, the "carriers of skull begging bowls".
  - Ritual sex with an unattractive, old or low-caste partner while both are visualized as the wrathful deities **Bhairava** and **Bhairavi**, then consuming the fluids as an offering, breaks **ascetic celibacy** and social norms.

### The Vijnana Bhairava Tantra and non-duality (slides 16 to 18)
- The **Vijnana Bhairava Tantra** is a Hindu tantra offering **112 methods** for opening the mind to the "ultimate reality". Its title means "the Wrathful Divine Consciousness".
- In it, Shiva in his wrathful form, Bhairava, instructs the Goddess in her wrathful form, Bhairavi, as a conversation between lovers.
- Shiva/Bhairava personifies universal consciousness, and the Goddess/Bhairavi personifies the material universe. Their message is that consciousness and the world are inseparable, "as if in sexual union or even closer".
- Shiva tells the initiate that her consciousness is not hers but his: a universal consciousness with no gender that is never born and never dies.
- Seeing oneself as a person separate from the universe is an illusion, a wave believing it is separate from the ocean. Awakening is the wave realizing it is part of the ocean.
- Every transgressive method, and the frightening look of the wrathful deities, is "shock therapy" to wake a person to this reality.
- Sex is one such method. It is transgressive, so it must be kept secret, and transformational, because it uses the very attachment that binds a person to samsara as the way out.
- The goddess **Chinnamasta**, who severed her own head and drinks her own blood, is a coded image of ego death in an awakening experience.

### Origins (slides 19 and 21)
- The origins are obscure, but many scholars place them in **Kashmir**, in northern India, around the **3rd to 4th centuries CE**, for both Hindu and Buddhist tantra.
- Hindu tantra then spread across the Indian subcontinent and into Southeast Asia and Indonesia.
- Esoteric Buddhism today is found mainly in **Tibet (78.5%)**, **Mongolia (51.7%)** and **Buryatia in Russian Siberia (20%)**, and elsewhere as a special case of Mahayana.

## Esoteric Buddhism: Vajrayana

### Name and status (slide 22)
- Esoteric Buddhism is also called **Vajrayana**, the Indestructible or Diamond Vehicle.
- A *vajra* is a thunderbolt, the indestructible divine weapon of Indian mythology.
- It is "a special case of Mahayana": its followers accept the Buddhist tantras as scripture equal or superior to the Mahayana sutras.

### Tantra in a monastery (slides 23 to 25)
- Buddhist tantra is unique because it is taught in a monastic setting, while Hindu tantra is mostly non-celibate.
- Monastics usually only visualize sexual union, with imagined deities called **wisdom partners** (Sanskrit *jnana mudra*, "a wisdom symbol"), so the transgression is symbolic. There are exceptions.
- Initiation is secret, and monastics may not discuss it with the uninitiated.
- The deity **Kalacakra** is visualized by initiated monastics. The Dalai Lama, who is celibate, practices this tantra and gives its initiation often.
- Initiation is a long ritual in which the master introduces the deity visualization, the deity's pure land, the **mantras** (sacred formulas) and the **mudras** (sacred gestures).
- In practice, monastics generate erotic pleasure in visualization, heightened by abstinence, and channel it in meditation toward awakening.

### Transforming the three poisons (slides 26 to 31)
- Exoteric schools use antidotes that **renounce** the three poisons (ignorance, passion, aversion): meditation on impermanence (Theravada) or emptiness (Mahayana), meditation on corpses, the monastic precepts, and meditation on loving kindness and compassion.
- Tantra tries instead to **transcend** the three poisons by using them, transforming them into divine energies. The saying is "passions are awakening" (Japanese *bonno soku bodai*).
- In an awakened mind each poison is an enlightened energy.
  - Ignorance is a peaceful, empty state of mind.
  - Passion is the blissful, approaching side of the awakened mind.
  - Anger is the liberating, radiant side that subdues obstacles.
- The deck gives one visualization for each poison.

| Poison | Deity visualized | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Ignorance (feeling trapped in self versus objects) | Buddha **Vairocana**, symbol of the enlightened universe | The blue body grows to engulf the universe, giving borderless emptiness and "extended subjectivity", then shrinks and vanishes. |
| Passion (overwhelming sexual desire) | Deities in sexual union, such as **Vajrasattva** and his consort | Both fill with the nectar of pleasure, which flows out and fills the world with bliss; good and bad "have one taste". |
| Anger | A wrathful deity, such as **Acala** (Japanese *Fudo Myoo*) in his mandala of fire | The causes of anger are consumed by the fire of wisdom, as in the Japanese **goma** fire ritual, until self and world are luminous emptiness. |

- Each visualization ends the same way: the practitioner opens her eyes and sees the world "as it is", free of dualistic constructs.

### Mind, speech and body (slide 32)
- Tantric ritual works on all three dimensions of a person, and a master must initiate the person into each.
  - Mind: **visualization** of oneself as the deity and the world as the deity's pure land (**mandala**).
  - Speech: **chanting** the secret formula (**mantra**).
  - Body: the sacred **gestures** (**mudra**).

### The subtle body (slides 33 to 36)
- Tantra treats the body as both gross and subtle, made of forms of energy.
- There are three main energy channels.

| Channel | Colour | Energy | When it dominates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central | Blue | Enters only in enlightened experience (partly), orgasm with a partner (fully) and death (fully) | When closed, ignorance reigns. |
| Left | Red | Lunar, blood, feminine, most active in sleep | Passion stirs the mind. |
| Right | White | Solar, semen, masculine, most active awake | Anger rises. |

- The whole tantric path aims at getting the solar and lunar energies into the central channel.
  - A visualized partner gets them in only partly.
  - An actual partner who is also a tantrika can bring full liberation in this life.
  - Monks and nuns must wait for death for the energies to enter fully, or disrobe and become lay tantrikas.
- The places where the three channels intertwine are the **chakras**. Intercourse "pierces" the chakra at the base of the central channel.

### Summary of the tantric path (slide 37)
- Tantras are esoteric and secret because they work with transgression, imaginary for monastics and real for lay tantrikas.
- The path needs initiation from a qualified master into a deity's visualization, mantras and gestures.
- The three poisons are not renounced, as in exoteric Buddhism or Vedic Hinduism, but transformed into what they really are, qualities of an awakened mind.
- Practice works on subtle energies, aiming to get them into the central channel partly (solo) or fully (orgasm or death).

## Monks, sex and abuse

### Padmasambhava and Tsongkhapa (slides 38 to 40)
- **Padmasambhava** (c. 8th century CE) came to Tibet from Kashmir (or Uddiyana) and was the first to bring Vajrayana there.
- He is said to have been a monastic who secretly practiced intercourse and reached full liberation while alive, producing the **rainbow body**: Tibetans believe his gross body dissolved into light, and he set up his pure land on the mythical **Copper Mountain**.
- Three of the four major Tibetan schools accept that accomplished tantrikas may stay monastics and practice sex.
- The **Gelug** school, founded by **Tsongkhapa (1357–1419)**, rejects this and enforces strict monasticism. Tsongkhapa said sexual practice is dangerous and can lead back to samsara, so he chose to wait for death for full liberation.
- The **Dalai Lamas** belong to the Gelug school.

### Monasticism and its tensions (slides 41 to 44)
- Vajrayana monasticism is as strict about sex as Theravada and monastic Mahayana, and it also inherits the view of women as karmically inferior, though in tantra the practitioner's sex should not matter.
- **Tenzin Palmo**, an English woman, became a nun in a Tibetan tradition; the deck uses her story for the tension between exoteric and esoteric practice.
- The stress on monasticism also answers sex scandals in Western tantric communities. **Chogyam Trungpa Rinpoche**, one of Tenzin Palmo's teachers, suffered from alcoholism and abused his students, and his disciple and son had scandals too.
- The lesson the deck draws, echoing Tsongkhapa: sex can lead back to attachment, especially without consent in an unequal master and disciple relationship, and such cases question the openly transgressive path.
- The deck's medieval Japanese example of how far antinomian practice went is the ritual abuse of temple boys (*chigo*), in which the boy was treated as a manifestation of Kannon. The deck calls it repugnant to modern moral sentiment.

### When the goal is forgotten (slides 45 to 47)
- The methods only point to the nature of mind, "empty, luminous, and blissful" awareness.
- Tantra can decay into stifling ritualism and self-serving motives, and secrecy breeds nepotism and elitism once the ruling class takes it up, as with medieval Japan's **cloistered emperors**.
- Japanese Zen, Pure Land and Nichiren Buddhism gained popularity partly from dissatisfaction with tantric elitism and antisocial transgression.
- **Emperor Go-Shirakawa (1127–1192)** was a typical cloistered emperor who kept ruling after abdicating, using tantric rituals and secret alliances.
- **Sohei** were warrior monks who used wrathful tantric practices in martial training and protected their temples, which were often burnt in warfare; many were swords for hire. **Benkei** is the typical sohei.
