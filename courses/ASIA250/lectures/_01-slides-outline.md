## Main topics, in slide order
1. **Hook: Hyon Gak** (b. 1964, born Paul Muenzen NJ, Catholic → Korean Seon monk, now teaches in Germany).
   His syllogism: religion = believing in something outside oneself / no creator God in Buddhism / so Buddhism is not a religion. Slides argue this fails.
2. **Defining "religion"** — abstract concepts resist definition (the "define love" thought experiment; "love is a rose" metaphor → myths work poetically).
   - Naive definition: narratives about the supernatural + rituals interacting with it. Problem: can't distinguish from "worldview"/"cultural knowledge".
   - **Course definition (memorise):** *a set of articulated and reenacted solutions (narratives and rituals) to the human predicament, premised on the ideas of and interaction with the supernatural, adhered to by a community in some institutionalized form.*
   - Evolutionary framing: self-awareness → human predicament (mortality, suffering, evil, injustice) → "solutions" via the supernatural: **agents** (gods, spirits, soul), **states** (nirvana, heaven, hell, afterlife), **cosmic justice** (karma, redemption, damnation).
   - Applied back to Hyon Gak's own claims (Buddha mind = agent; nirvana = state; attachment→suffering = cosmic justice), so Buddhism *is* a religion by this definition.
   - **Religious practice** = sustained effort to base behaviour on ideas/experiences of the supernatural, by internalising narratives and ritualising daily life.
3. **Narrative / story** (synonyms): a meaningful, interrelated sequence of events; always selective, so it reinforces values. Includes material culture (books, art, objects = memory aids).
4. **Myth** = a form of **allegory** (needs interpretation for hidden moral/political meaning). Myths express the WHY (origins, identity: "a story they tell themselves about themselves") and WHAT IS TRUE (truth claims in symbolic form); not history/science. **Mythology** = study of myths / a group's collected myths.
   - Two interpretive orientations: (1) disregard content (reductive), (2) take content seriously (contextualise bizarre elements). Powell 1995 quote on Greek intellectuals; interpretation resolves dissonance.
   - Worked example: **Buddha's birth story** (born from mother's side = free of attachment; 7 steps = a god born human; "I alone am the holy one" = retrospective claim of superiority over Hindu gods).
   - Worked example: **Rigveda Purusha creation myth** — primordial being self-disintegrates into the universe; gods and humans keep sacrificing him. Answer deferred to lecture 2. Video transposes Vedic meter into English. Reading of a text as ritual = **liturgy**. Sacrifice modelled on a feast/gift exchange with guests from another tribe.
   - Purusha myth = **panentheism** (God greater than, includes and interpenetrates the universe). Sacrificial system maintains the **faith frame** (Luhrmann 2020). Sacrifice = metaphorical extension of tribal gift-exchange economies.
5. **Ritual** = "a complex of behavior and language whose performance brings about an effect, transforming the participants or their environment" (before–during–after; like a journey). Rituals have a **script**, a **purpose**, and work within a **cosmology**. **Ritual studies** = field in anthropology + religious studies; examples debated: oaths, funerals, rites of passage, greetings/etiquette, music, hospitality.
   - Purpose of religious practice = ritualise every aspect of life → everyday world becomes a **paracosm** (Luhrmann 2020).
6. **World religions** = religions with diasporic presence / that cross state borders. Buddhism: ~500–535 million followers, >7% of world, >99% in Asia-Pacific, a minority in its homeland (Nepal/India).
7. **Buddhism experientially: axiological aesthetics** (Shooklyn 2023). Axiology = value theory. Aesthetic experience = putting a frame over something and sustaining attention on it; not limited to art. Two-drivers sunset example (Driver A traffic-fixated, Driver B pulls over, hypothetical Driver C "God is punishing me"). Chain: aesthetic experience → **value formation** → behaviour. Values def (Schwartz 1994): preferred ways of acting/being that serve as guiding principles.
   - **Faith frame / paracosm** produced by (1) narratives, (2) rituals, (3) religious objects — attempt to detect the supernatural in the natural.
   - **Jones (1979) facets of aesthetic experience:** formal, primeval/archetypal, affective, sentimental, extrinsic, iconic, cognitive, sterile/technical — with a parallel "via a faith frame" column (supernatural agents/states, monk/shaman embodiment, suffering/ecstasy, group bonding, "ask the deity for X", symbolic gesture, "fits concept X", "religion Y does X on such a day").
   - Meme: Buddhist nun sees Jesus in her latte.
8. **Schools by the 1st c. CE** — CONSERVATIVE (→ modern Theravada): aim = nirvana; **arhat** = liberated follower, not reborn, free of the **3 poisons** (anger, passion, ignorance); one Buddha per universe at a time. PROGRESSIVE (→ most of the Buddhist world): follower declares as a **bodhisattva** (future Buddha) to lead others; Buddha creates his own **Buddha Land** where bodhisattvas are reborn to train; multiple universes = faster buddhahood elsewhere.
