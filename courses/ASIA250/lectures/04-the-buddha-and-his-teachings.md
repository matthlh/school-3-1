## Slides, organized

### The 12 Great Deeds and the Lalitavistara
- Every Buddha is said to accomplish the same **12 Great Deeds**.
- This lecture picks up at deed 7 and runs to deed 12.
- The title **Lalitavistara** means "the extensive play".
- In that title, lalita means play or divine sport, and vistara means extensive.

### Deed 7: leaving the palace
- After he sees suffering, Siddhartha leaves the palace with his charioteer **Channa**.
- In the forest he cuts his hair and removes his princely clothes.
- He becomes a wandering ascetic, a **shramana**.
- He leaves behind his wife and his newborn son.
- He names the son **Rahula**, which means "stumbling block".

### The brahmanical counter-view: duty and the four stages of life
- The **Bhagavadgita** shows the brahmanical view that renunciation is set against.
- In it, Krishna tells the warrior Arjuna to fight.
- The first reason is that fighting is Arjuna's caste duty, his **dharma**.
- The second reason is that the **atman** (the soul) cannot be destroyed.
- Brahmanical life has four stages, the **ashramas**:
  - brahmacarya, youth and study;
  - grihastha, the householder;
  - vanaprastha, retiring to the forest;
  - sannyasa, renunciation.
- The first two stages affirm the world, and the last one renounces it.

### Deed 8: two teachers, eight absorptions, six years of asceticism
- In deed 8 Siddhartha studies under two teachers.
- Under **Alara Kalama** he masters the four meditative absorptions, the **dhyanas** (Pali **jhanas**).
- The fourth absorption gives a fully equanimous mind, but it is not liberation.
- Instead, the fourth absorption leads to rebirth in the **world of form**, where gods called Brahma live.
- Three words must be kept apart: **Brahma** is a god, a **Brahmin** is a priest, and **Brahman** is the absolute.
- Under **Udraka Ramaputra** he learns subtler states that lead into the **formless realm**.
- The four absorptions from Alara Kalama and the four from Udraka Ramaputra make eight stages of absorption.
- The eight stages resemble **samadhi** in Classical Yoga.
- Patanjali's **Yoga Sutras** are dated in the deck to about the 2nd century BCE.
- The Yoga Sutras define yoga as the cessation of the fluctuations of the mind.
- In that system, the quiet mind reflects the atman.
- Siddhartha then spends six years in strict asceticism and nearly starves.

### Why he turns to the middle way
- The first story is the lute string: wound too tight it snaps, and left too loose it makes no sound.
- The second story is muddy water, which clears only when it is left to settle.
- Both stories point him to the **middle way**.

### Buddhist cosmology
- Buddhist cosmology can be mapped onto the meditator's body, with **Mount Meru** as the backbone.
- The realms rise in three levels:
  - the **desire realms**, characterised by "pleasure";
  - the **form realms**, characterised by "clarity";
  - the **formless realms**, characterised by "space".
- Meditation through these realms was like a shamanic journey.

### Deed 9: victory over Mara
- **Mara** is the god of passion and sexual desire.
- The deck also calls him **Kamadeva**.
- His name means "death".
- He rules the desire realm from its highest heaven.
- Mara's daughters personify the passions.
- Mara's army personifies anger.
- The story reworks the Hindu myth in which Shiva burns Kama with his third eye.
- Unlike Shiva, Siddhartha conquers Mara without killing him.
- Bertolucci's film **Little Buddha** (1993) shows this battle as a story told to children within the film.

### Deed 10: awakening under the Bodhi tree
- He sits under the sacred fig, the **Bodhi tree**, and awakens at dawn.
- The awakening comes as three realisations.
- First, he remembers all his previous lives, which yields the teaching of the 29 Buddhas.
- Second, after stopping his mind in the eighth absorption, he realises that there is no atman. This is **anatman**, "no soul".
- What exists instead of a soul is two kinds of matter.
- Physical matter is made of five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space.
- Conscious matter is made of the five **skandhas**: form, sensation, perception, volition and cognition.
- The skandhas are impermanent.
- They pass from body to body like fire jumping from branch to branch.
- On this view, Hinduism mistakes the fire's changes for a permanent substance.
- Third, he realises that only **nirvana**, literally "blowing out", is final peace.
- Final nirvana comes when his last body dies.
- Dhammapada verses 153 and 154 address the "house-builder", which is craving, as seen and defeated.

### The three marks (as the deck lists them)
- The deck lists the three marks, the **lakshanas**, as anatman (no soul), **anitya** (impermanence) and nirvana.
- The deck describes nirvana as the end of **dukkha** (suffering).

### After the awakening
- He stays under the tree for 49 days.
- 49 days is also the time the mind of the dead lingers before rebirth.
- He is 35 years old.
- He is now the Buddha, "the awakened one".

### Deed 11: teaching
- "Turning the wheel", the **dharmachakra**, is the metaphor for teaching.
- Dharma now means the truth spoken by the Buddha, not caste duty.
- In the plural, **dharmas** are the constituents of existence, like atoms.
- He teaches for 45 years.
- He teaches people of every caste.
- His followers form four groups: monks (**bhikshus**), nuns (**bhikshunis**), laymen and laywomen.

### How the teaching was preserved
- Buddhists had no writing for over 200 years.
- The **Pali Canon** is in Pali, a dialect of western India from about the 3rd century BCE.
- The first Pali inscriptions date to about 25 BCE, more than 400 years after the Buddha.
- The full canon took its current form in the 5th century CE.

### Deed 12: death and relics
- He dies after eating spoiled pork given to him as an offering.
- Buddhists hold that he chose to die in order to teach impermanence.
- His cremated bone fragments became relics, **sharira**.
- The relics were enshrined in **stupas**.
