## Questions

### Q: Two people freely promise to care for each other. Years later one keeps showing up through a difficult period because the relationship matters. How does B understand the role of duty here?
- (a) Duty gives the partner a way to keep living out their love through the difficulty.
- (b) Duty takes over from love whenever keeping the promise becomes difficult.
- (c) Duty adds value chiefly because others can hold the couple to the promise.
- (d) Duty restores the intensity the partners felt when they first met.
**A:** Key:
1. (a) Duty gives the couple ways to sustain the love they brought to marriage.

B treats the duty of marriage as truly loving. The vow gives the couple a way to act on their love through time and hardship. [Duty and the end of the letter](readings/aesthetic-validity.md#duty-and-the-end-of-the-letter)

### Q: Someone already wants to live honestly and must decide whether to conceal a mistake. Which choice is this for B?
- (a) The first either/or, which asks whether good and evil matter as categories at all.
- (b) The aesthetic choice, which concerns which possibility feels more interesting.
- (c) The absolute either/or, which concerns good and evil in the action itself.
- (d) The initial choice to accept a concrete self with an existing history.
**A:** Key:
1. (c) The absolute either/or is the choice between good and evil once the person has taken up the ethical.

B’s first either/or concerns whether the person will let good and evil guide their life. Choosing to conceal or admit the mistake comes after that question. [The two choices](readings/equilibrium.md#the-choice-and-what-philosophy-cannot-do)

### Q: A couple proposes a marriage that either spouse can end by giving notice once the passion has faded. Which response to romantic love does this illustrate?
- (a) The marriage of reason, since the couple has planned for a practical difficulty.
- (b) The immoral path, since the arrangement keeps passion and makes the union depend on it.
- (c) B’s idea of the vow, since the couple willingly enters the arrangement.
- (d) B’s idea of a higher concentricity, since the arrangement keeps the attraction intact.
**A:** Key:
1. (b) The immoral path keeps the attraction while protecting an exit when it fades.

B treats the limited arrangement as a protest against marriage. The marriage of reason takes the other path and bases marriage on practical calculation. [First love and the reflective age](readings/aesthetic-validity.md#first-love-and-the-reflective-age)

### Q: An athlete enjoys a successful career and makes winning the sole measure of a worthwhile life. Then the wins stop. Why would B say the athlete was already in despair?
- (a) The athlete was necessarily sad underneath the enjoyment.
- (b) Success eventually becomes boring for anyone who enjoys it.
- (c) The athlete knew exactly when the career would stop going well.
- (d) The athlete’s sense of worth already rested entirely on something that could change.
**A:** Key:
1. (d) The losses exposed a dependence that was there even during the wins.

B’s claim concerns what the life is built on. The condition was transitory while it held, too. [Every aesthetic life is despair](readings/equilibrium.md#every-aesthetic-life-is-despair)

### Q: A couple makes their vow publicly and keeps the details of their relationship private with friends. One partner hides a serious concern from the other to preserve the feeling of mystery. How would B assess their choices?
- (a) Both kinds of secrecy help the couple protect first love.
- (b) Privacy with friends can protect the relationship, and openness between the partners helps it grow.
- (c) The public vow requires the couple to be equally open with friends and with each other.
- (d) The couple should preserve mystery between themselves and seek openness with the wider community.
**A:** Key:
1. (b) B values privacy with outsiders and openness between spouses.

A’s imagined separate wings keep the spouses mysterious to each other. B thinks getting to know the person is what makes deeper love possible. [Privacy outside and honesty inside](readings/aesthetic-validity.md#how-marriage-gains-beauty-over-time)

### Q: A reader concludes from “the good is by virtue of my willing it” that preferences determine what is right. Which response fits B’s argument on Hannay p. 524?
- (a) Willing brings a person into relation with good and evil, and the categories have absolute validity.
- (b) Good and evil apply only to the choices an individual personally approves of.
- (c) Good and evil are relative categories because thought can compare only relative differences.
- (d) Thought establishes the good through necessity, so the will adds nothing to the distinction.
**A:** Key:
1. (a) Choosing makes the distinction matter in how one lives. B claims absolute validity for the distinction itself.

B gives thought relative comparisons and puts ethical commitment in the sphere of freedom. A person’s willing is what lets the distinction govern how they live. [Lecture 22 on p. 524](lectures/22-equilibrium-hegel-and-the-choice-of-good-and-evil.md#what-b-is-saying-on-hannay-p-524)

### Q: Someone devotes themselves to loving God and concludes that their family and civic relationships are obstacles to a purer life. Which criticism would B make?
- (a) They first need to discover which talent gives them the most pleasure.
- (b) They should follow the people around them more closely when judging every obligation.
- (c) They choose themselves abstractly by leaving out the actual relationships in which their responsibilities lie.
- (d) They should perfect their inner life before deciding what they owe the people around them.
**A:** Key:
1. (c) The mystic’s choice leaves out the concrete life the person actually has.

B sees ethical repentance as taking the person back into the world they belong to. The mystic’s withdrawal takes them out of it. [The ethical life-view](readings/equilibrium.md#the-ethical-life-view)
