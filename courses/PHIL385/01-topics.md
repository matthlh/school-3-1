## Look-alikes

Topics that are easy to mix up. When a quiz picks a question from one, a question from its look-alike comes right after it.

| Topic | Look-alike | Why they get confused |
|---|---|---|
| The gallery in The Unhappiest One | Ancient Tragedy's Reflection in the Modern | Antigone is in both essays, as a candidate in the gallery of The Unhappiest One and as A's modern Antigone in Ancient Tragedy, so what A says of her in one gets put in the other. |
| The unhappy consciousness and the three formations | Crop Rotation | Both essays turn on memory and hope, but Crop Rotation's art of remembering and forgetting is a method against boredom, while The Unhappiest One's rememberer and hoper are people absent from themselves. |
| The Unhappiest One | Kraal on The Unhappiest One | Both describe how the essay ends, but the happiest, a gift of fortune and envy are A's own words, while doxology, paradox and comfort are Kraal's words for it. |
| First authorship: the 8 pseudonymous works | Second authorship | Johannes Climacus signs Philosophical Crumbs and the Postscript in the first authorship, while Anti-Climacus signs The Sickness unto Death and Practice in Christianity in the second, and the years run on without a gap, 1843 to 1846 and then 1847 to 1855. |
| Either/Or Preface | How Either/Or is put together | Both cover the book's title and the order of A's and B's papers, so what Eremita says in the Preface and what the printed volumes contain get mixed. |
| Kierkegaard 1841–43 | Kierkegaard's family losses | The engagement to Regine ends in October 1841, two weeks before he leaves for Berlin and in the year of his dissertation, so the family and engagement dates get mixed with the 1841 to 1843 ones. |
| Post-Hegelian context | Kierkegaard 1841–43 | Both are timelines that end at Schelling's call to Berlin in 1841, so the dates of the Hegel dispute, 1785 to 1837, get mixed with Kierkegaard's own 1841 to 1843. |
| The Musical Erotic: Don Giovanni | The Musical Erotic: the three stages | Don Giovanni is the third stage in both, so lecture 11's points (woman in general, the catalogue, the replaceable women) get mixed with the stage summary from lecture 9. |
| The Musical Erotic: Plato, Christianity and the demonic | The Musical Erotic: why music | Music as a Christian art appears in both: lecture 9 has A's page 75 claim, lecture 12 has Kraal's Greek-versus-Christian contrast and the demonic, so the two get merged. |
| The Seducer's Diary: Johannes and Cordelia | The Musical Erotic: Don Giovanni | Both are seducers in A's papers, so what is said of one gets said of the other. Don Giovanni is immediate and musical, desires woman in general and keeps a catalogue; Johannes is reflective and calculating, plans one woman's seduction in prose, and wants her free gift of herself. |
| Diapsalmata: moralizing and the meaning of life | The Seducer's Diary: Johannes and Cordelia | The Diapsalmata lecture starts from the diary, so the moralizing point gets attached to Johannes. Moralizing about Johannes is what the Diapsalmata make impossible, because A has no standard of how to live, and the diary's own point is the free gift and the resistance paradox. |
| The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: marriage's history, habit and duty | Diapsalmata: moralizing and the meaning of life | B quotes A's "do it or do not do it, you will regret both" from the Diapsalmata as the result of taking duty to be love's enemy, so who says the line and where it comes from get mixed. |
| The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: first love and the reflective age | The Musical Erotic: the three stages | B uses Mozart's Page as the dreaming state before first love and defines romantic love as immediate, so A's three stages and B's first love get merged; for B first love is awake and aimed at one definite person, unlike the dreaming Page. |
| The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: B's task and tone | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: marriage's history, habit and duty | Kraal's argument that love is not the true victor if duty is its enemy comes from the duty section at the end of the essay, so the lecture's framing and B's own duty-is-love's-friend passage get mixed. |
| The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: the ethical as the more aesthetic | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: first love and the reflective age | Both cover love, lust and the stamp of the eternal, so Kraal's five-step diagram gets mixed with B's own account of romantic and first love. The diagram's note says romantic love seeks to transform into married love, while B says first love enters marriage without being changed and calls the result its transfiguration, the same love kept and made more glorious. |
| Equilibrium: Hegel's system and the choice of good and evil | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: the ethical as the more aesthetic | Lecture 19 ends with the reading of B as a Hegelian, and lecture 22 has B break with Hegel: he grants mediation in logic, nature and history but denies that good and evil are a category of thought, so what B takes from Hegel and what he refuses get mixed. |
| Equilibrium: despair and choosing oneself | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: first love and the reflective age | Melancholy is in both letters: in the first it is the age's fear of committing, egoistic or sympathetic, while in Equilibrium it is hysteria of the spirit, Nero's nature and a cardinal sin by one's own fault, so the two accounts get merged. |
| Equilibrium: despair and choosing oneself | Equilibrium: Hegel's system and the choice of good and evil | Both are about B's choice: the Hegel topic has its logic, choosing to will rather than between good and evil and thought against freedom, while the despair topic has its content, oneself in one's eternal validity reached through despair and repentance, so what is chosen and why it cannot be thought get swapped. |
| Equilibrium: the false paths, the mystic and the aesthetic's return | Diapsalmata: moralizing and the meaning of life | In the Diapsalmata lecture A has no belief about the meaning of life, while in Equilibrium B pins on A the view that sorrow is the meaning of life and the unhappiest is the happiest, and calls it despair and fatalism, so A's own position and B's description of it get mixed. |
| Equilibrium: the false paths, the mystic and the aesthetic's return | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: the ethical as the more aesthetic | Both argue that the ethical is the more beautiful, but lecture 19 has Kraal's five steps and the Republic's beautiful-from-the-good, while Equilibrium has B's own argument that A's beauty has no movement and only the ethical individual has an immanent teleology. |
| Equilibrium: the ethical individual, duty, calling and work | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: marriage's history, habit and duty | Duty is in both letters: in the first it is love's friend and the conductor's baton, while in Equilibrium it is inside the individual rather than outside, Pligt as what imposes, and every man's duty to work, so which letter says what about duty gets confused. |
| Equilibrium: marriage, friendship and the uncommon man | The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage: B's task and tone | Marriage is argued in both letters: the first defends its aesthetic validity and shows how it keeps first love, while Equilibrium makes it every man's duty because he who marries realizes the universal, with the relationship as the absolute and ethics silent on whom, so the two arguments get attributed to the wrong letter. |
