## Lec 1 — Overview of the "Authorship" (Fri Sep 11, logged 2026-09-11)

### Q: In what year did Hegel die, and what two camps did his followers split into afterwards? What separated them?
**Topic:** Post-Hegelian context  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** 1831. Right (Old) Hegelians read the system as compatible with orthodox Christianity (personal God, immortality); Left (Young) Hegelians (Strauss, Feuerbach, Bauer, later Marx) read it as incompatible with theism. The right/left labels themselves come from this dispute (Strauss, 1837).

### Q: Name the author, title and date of the book that set off the theological dispute over Hegel, and what it claimed about the Gospels.
**Topic:** Post-Hegelian context  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** David Friedrich Strauss, *The Life of Jesus, Critically Examined* (*Das Leben Jesu*), 1835–36. The Gospel narratives are myth produced by the early Christian community, not history. A Left Hegelian reading.

### Q: Why did calling Hegel a pantheist amount to calling him an atheist in Germany, and how did Hegel describe his own religious position?
**Topic:** Post-Hegelian context  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** Since the Pantheism Controversy (Jacobi, 1785), pantheism (God = the whole, no personal God) was treated as atheism in disguise. Critics said Hegel's Absolute Spirit is impersonal, hence pantheist, hence atheist. Hegel said he was and would remain a Lutheran and presented his philosophy as Christian philosophy.

### Q: Title, year and subject of Kierkegaard's dissertation?
**Topic:** Kierkegaard 1841–43  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** *On the Concept of Irony with Continual Reference to Socrates*, 1841 (defended 29 Sept), his master's (magister) dissertation at Copenhagen, on Socratic irony.

### Q: Who was called to Berlin in 1841 by the King of Prussia, what was the task in the king's own metaphor, and what did Kierkegaard have to do with it?
**Topic:** Kierkegaard 1841–43  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** F. W. J. Schelling, called by Friedrich Wilhelm IV to "stamp out the dragon-seed of Hegelian pantheism," i.e. to defend Christian philosophy against Hegelianism. Kierkegaard went to Berlin (Oct 1841) and attended the lectures over winter 1841–42; he was enthusiastic at first, then disappointed, and was back in Copenhagen by March 1842.

### Q: What was Kierkegaard's public debut as an author, in what year, and under whose name did it appear?
**Topic:** Kierkegaard 1841–43  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** *Either/Or* (*Enten–Eller*), February 1843, "edited by Victor Eremita" — the papers of "A" (the aesthete) and "B" (Judge William).

### Q: List the eight pseudonymous works of the first authorship in publication order, with years.
**Topic:** First authorship: the 8 pseudonymous works  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** Either/Or (1843) · Fear and Trembling (1843) · Repetition (1843) · Philosophical Crumbs/Fragments (1844) · The Concept of Anxiety (1844) · Prefaces (1844) · Stages on Life's Way (1845) · Concluding Unscientific Postscript (1846).

### Q: Match each pseudonym to its work: Victor Eremita, Johannes de silentio, Constantin Constantius, Johannes Climacus, Vigilius Haufniensis, Nicolaus Notabene, Hilarius Bookbinder.
**Topic:** First authorship: the 8 pseudonymous works  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** Victor Eremita → Either/Or (editor) · Johannes de silentio → Fear and Trembling · Constantin Constantius → Repetition · Johannes Climacus → Philosophical Crumbs and the Postscript · Vigilius Haufniensis → The Concept of Anxiety · Nicolaus Notabene → Prefaces · Hilarius Bookbinder → Stages on Life's Way (editor).

### Q: Which work is associated with the "leap of faith" and the Abraham/Isaac story? Year and pseudonym?
**Topic:** First authorship: the 8 pseudonymous works  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** *Fear and Trembling*, 1843, by Johannes de silentio.

### Q: Why do Philosophical Crumbs and the Concluding Unscientific Postscript share a pseudonym? Who is he and what is his standpoint?
**Topic:** First authorship: the 8 pseudonymous works  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** The Postscript is the sequel: its full title is *Concluding Unscientific Postscript to Philosophical Crumbs*. Both are by Johannes Climacus, who writes as a non-Christian asking how one becomes a Christian. The Postscript (1846) was intended to conclude the authorship.

### Q: What is the relation between the views in a pseudonymous book and Kierkegaard's own views, and where does he say so himself?
**Topic:** Pseudonymity and the 18 discourses  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** They are not his views: each pseudonym is a perspective on existence thought through from inside. In "A First and Last Explanation" (appended to the Postscript, 1846) he admits authorship but says there is "not a single word" of his own in the pseudonymous books and asks that quotations be attributed to the pseudonym. Attributing a pseudonym's position to Kierkegaard is the standard misreading, even among scholars.

### Q: What did Kierkegaard publish under his own name during the first authorship, how many, and how did they relate to the pseudonymous books?
**Topic:** Pseudonymity and the 18 discourses  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** Upbuilding (edifying) discourses: eighteen, in six small volumes (1843–44), each issued alongside a pseudonymous book. They carry his own religious standpoint while the pseudonyms carry the alternative perspectives. "Upbuilding," not sermons, because he was not ordained.

### Q: Name the main works of the second authorship with years, and say which are signed and which are pseudonymous.
**Topic:** Second authorship  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** *Works of Love* (1847, signed) · *Christian Discourses* (1848, signed) · *The Sickness unto Death* (1849) and *Practice in Christianity* (1850), both by the pseudonym Anti-Climacus with "S. Kierkegaard" as editor · *The Point of View for My Work as an Author* (written 1848, published 1859). The second authorship presents his own religious perspective, unlike the first.

### Q: Why does the second authorship centre on love, and what distinction does Works of Love draw between kinds of love?
**Topic:** Second authorship  **Lec:** 1  **Type:** recall
**A:** Love is the closest thing to the faith he wants to hold in his heart. Preferential love (erotic love, friendship, love of country) loves the other as "another self," so it is self-love in disguise. Christian neighbour-love is commanded ("you shall love"), non-preferential, loves the neighbour as yourself, with God as the "middle term."
