## The essay, start to finish
In my words, checked against the Danish at tekster.kb.dk and the Søren Kierkegaard Research
Centre's commentary. Short phrases in quotation marks are the wording worth carrying into an exam.

### Title and epigraph
- The full heading is "Crop Rotation. An attempt at a theory of social prudence." Hong's edition
  says "Rotation of Crops: A Venture in a Theory of Social Prudence"; same essay.
- The epigraph is from Aristophanes' *Plutus*: Chremylos says one finally gets sick of everything,
  of love, music, honour, courage, ambition, command, and his slave Karion answers each with a
  food. Satiety is the note the essay opens on.
- It is A's seventh piece, the last before "The Seducer's Diary", and the closest thing in the book
  to A's own programme for living.

### The principle, and the children
- A starts from a principle, because experienced people say that is sensible: all people are
  boring. The principle has the repelling force of "the negative", which in Hegel is the principle
  of motion; whoever has it behind him has infinite momentum.
- Then the sentence: boredom is a root of all evil. The Danish says *a* root, *en Rod*; your copy
  and your page say *the*. Either is fine in an answer, but know the original hedges.
- The proof from children: as long as children are amused they are good, and they turn unruly when
  boredom is on its way. So a nursemaid is hired partly on whether she can amuse them, and would be
  sacked for failing at it whatever her other virtues. That makes the nursemaid the one relation in
  life where aesthetics gets its due: nobody gets to divorce a boring wife, depose a boring king,
  exile a boring priest, sack a boring minister or execute a boring journalist. Habit and boredom
  have taken over, and that is why the world goes backwards.

### The history of the world as boredom
- The gods were bored, so they created human beings. Adam was bored alone, so Eve was created, and
  boredom entered the world and grew with the population: Adam alone, then Adam and Eve, then the
  family with Cain and Abel, then the peoples en masse.
- To amuse themselves they built a tower into the clouds, an idea as boring as the tower was tall.
  They were scattered, the way people now travel abroad, and stayed bored.
- What held off the fall of Rome was bread and circuses, the phrase on your page. Today, instead of
  finding a diversion, people hasten the fall: convening an assembly of the estates, paying down the
  national debt by economies.
- A's proposal: Denmark borrows millions and spends it all on public amusement, so everything is
  free, from the theatre to the funeral; Copenhagen becomes another Athens; the world's rich move
  in, and the emperor of Persia is kidnapped and sold to the Turk if Persia replaces him. The king
  should be whoever tells the best joke. Then he catches himself: his wisdom is not for everyone, he
  wants no disciples, and would at most whisper it to one person on his deathbed.
- Man is called a social animal; A says he is a beast of prey, and the talk of society is inherited
  hypocrisy and calculated cunning.

### Two classes of the boring
- "Boring" can mean one who bores others or one who bores himself. Those who bore others are the
  plebs, the crowd; those who bore themselves are the elect, the nobility. The odd thing is that the
  people who never bore themselves bore everyone else, and the people who bore themselves are
  entertaining. The busy are the worst.
- The nobility bore themselves so thoroughly that even at the peak of boredom they entertain the
  initiated, when they either, passively, die of boredom, or, actively, shoot themselves out of
  curiosity. That is your quote, and it is about the elect at their extreme, not a rule for
  everyone.

### Idleness is not the problem
- The proverb says idleness is a root of all evil and prescribes work. A calls that plebeian.
  Idleness is a divine life as long as one is not bored: the Olympian gods lived in happy idleness,
  and a beauty who neither sews nor spins nor irons nor reads nor plays is happy in idleness because
  she is not bored. A person with no sense for idleness has not risen to the human; there is a
  restless activity that puts a man among the animals, who must always be moving.
- "Idleness is the devil's pillow" is right, but the devil gets no time to lay his head on it if one
  is not bored. Because people think man's destiny is to work, they oppose idleness to work; A
  thinks man's destiny is to amuse himself, so he opposes boredom to amusement.
- "Boredom is the demonic pantheism." Pantheism means fullness; boredom is built on emptiness, and
  is pantheistic for that very reason, everywhere as nothing. If you stay in it, it is the evil;
  annulled, it is true; and only amusement annuls it, so one ought to amuse oneself. Work annuls
  idleness, its opposite, but cannot touch boredom. The busiest workers are the most boring, and if
  they are not bored it is only because they have no idea what boredom is.
- Boredom "rests on the nothing that winds through existence", and its dizziness is that of looking
  into an infinite abyss. The English are its paradigm nation: the travelling Englishman, a heavy
  immovable marmot whose entire language is one interjection. Their opposite number, the apostles of
  empty enthusiasm who shout at everything, are just as empty.

### The two kinds of rotation
- Everyone who is bored cries for change. A agrees, but on principle. His word is crop rotation, and
  it has two senses.
- The vulgar one is changing the soil: tired of the country, move to the city; tired of your
  country, go abroad; "tired of Europe", go to America; then dream of travelling from star to star.
  Or tired of eating off porcelain, eat off silver, then gold; then burn half of Rome to see Troy
  burn. A calls this **the bad infinity**, Hegel's term for an endless series that never arrives,
  and says the method cancels itself. What did Nero get? Marcus Aurelius was wiser: it is in your
  power to live again, see things again as you once saw them, and that is what living again is.
- A's own is the true rotation: keep the soil and change the method of cultivation and the crop.
  Here lies **the principle of limitation, the only saving principle in the world**. The more you
  limit yourself, the more inventive you become. The prisoner for life is endlessly inventive and a
  spider is great entertainment to him. Schooldays: a fly under a nutshell; a fly shut into a hole
  in the desk and watched through paper; the monotonous drip from the roof. The contrast is
  **extensive against intensive**: relief is sought not by extensity but by intensity.

### Remembering and forgetting
- Every change of method falls under the relation between remembering and forgetting, the two
  currents all of life moves in; the whole art is to have them in your power.
- First, throw hope overboard. Only then does one begin to live artistically, since as long as one
  hopes one cannot limit oneself. Hope is a faithless pilot, and one of Prometheus's dubious gifts:
  instead of the immortals' foreknowledge he gave men hope. This is the same A who wrote "The
  Unhappiest One", and hope is again the thing to be rid of.
- *Nil admirari*, Horace's "wonder at nothing", is the real wisdom of life. No moment should mean so
  much that you cannot forget it at will, and each should mean enough that you can recall it at
  will. Childhood remembers best and forgets easiest. To remember poetically is really to forget,
  because the experience has already shed everything painful.
- So never enjoy to the last drop: then you can neither remember nor forget, only suffer a surfeit.
  When enjoyment sweeps you off, stop and remember. Never set all sails. No weapon is as dangerous as
  the art of remembering, though the police only ban concealed ones.
- Forgetting is not forgetfulness. It should apply to the pleasant too, since the pleasant, once
  past, carries the unpleasantness of longing. Every misfortune can be flattered, since even
  Cerberus took honey cakes. Forgetting is "the scissors with which one cuts away what one cannot
  use, under the highest supervision of memory"; the two are identical, and that artificially
  produced identity is the Archimedean point that lifts the world. Then one can play shuttlecock
  with the whole of existence. A person's elasticity is measured by his power to forget.

### The three things to avoid
- All of this exists to keep you from running aground in any single relationship and to secure
  "perfect floating".
- **Friendship.** A friend is not what philosophy calls the necessary other but the superfluous
  third. The rites of friendship, drinking to first names, opening a vein, mixing blood; the ancient
  mark, to want and not want the same things, which is also extremely boring; and the truth that
  one person can be nothing for another except in the way. This is not a life without people. It is
  always having more speed than the relationship, so you can run from it. The farmer lets a field
  lie fallow; social prudence does the same, and everything comes round again in a new form.
- **Marriage.** Lovers promise love for ever, which is easy and means nothing; "till Easter" or
  "till next May Day" would at least mean something one might keep. Soon one party sees it was a
  mistake, the other cries faithlessness, then both reach the same point and their mutual
  faithlessness cancels to mutual contentment; but divorce is hard, so marriage has to be propped up
  with moral supports. Calling the man who wants out a scoundrel is an indirect attack on marriage:
  either it has reality and he is punished by losing it, or it has none and he is wiser than the
  rest. Never contract a relationship by which you become several. With a wife, ordering travelling
  boots is difficult; with wife and children, impossible. A gypsy woman once carried her husband on
  her back through life, but that is rare and tiring in the long run, for the man. And marriage
  lands you in a fatal continuity with custom, which is as unpredictable as the weather. Then the
  lines you flagged: woman is and remains man's ruin the moment one contracts a lasting relationship
  with her; a woman's love is only pretence and weakness; let a young man fiery as an Arab horse
  marry and he is lost.
- **An official post.** Take one and you become a mere Peter Somebody, a tiny peg in the machine of
  the state; you stop being the master of your own cultivation; you get a title, and a title you
  never lose again except by a crime that gets you flogged at the post, and even then a royal pardon
  may give it back. Not inactivity, though: cultivate every occupation identical with idleness, all
  the breadless arts, intensively rather than extensively. It takes little to please children.

### Arbitrariness, the whole secret
- One must vary oneself constantly; that is the secret. Moods cannot be produced at will, but
  prudence teaches one to use the moment, to see a mood coming the way a sailor sees a squall.
  Teasing is an excellent instrument of exploration.
- "The whole secret lies in arbitrariness." One enjoys not the thing but something quite different
  one arbitrarily reads into it: watch the middle of a play, read the third part of a book, and get a
  pleasure the author never intended. A's example is a man whose philosophical chatter he was
  obliged to hear; near despair he noticed the man sweated when he talked, watched the drops gather
  on his forehead, run down his nose and hang at the tip, and from then on could even egg him on to
  lecture, just to watch. Baggesen objected to a decent man that nothing rhymed with his name.
- Make something accidental into the absolute and admire it absolutely; treat all of life as a
  wager; keep changing the eye you see reality with. The degree of consistency shows whether one is
  an artist or a bungler. To arbitrariness inside corresponds the accidental outside: keep an open
  eye for it and always be ready, since pleasures prepared a fortnight ahead mean little and the
  most trivial accident can become rich material. No theory can go further; even the fullest theory
  is poverty next to what genius discovers on its own.

## Clarifications on your questions

### Idleness versus boredom, and your quote
- Idleness is doing nothing. Boredom is emptiness, and it can hit the busiest person alive. That is
  A's whole move against the proverb: the folk saying blames idleness and prescribes work, and A says
  work cures idleness, its opposite, but never boredom, and that the busiest workers are the most
  boring people of all. Idleness is fine, even divine, provided one is not bored.
- So your gloss, that the quote "emphasises idleness as the true remedy", is the wrong way round.
  Idleness is not a remedy for anything; it is the neutral condition in which one is either bored or
  amused. The remedy for boredom is amusement, brought about by rotation.
- And the quote itself is not a thesis about everyone. It is the extreme case of the noble class,
  the ones who bore themselves: at the top of their boredom they either die of it or shoot
  themselves out of curiosity.

### "Embrace boredom through routine" — no, the opposite
- A never embraces boredom. It is the root of evil and has to be annulled, and only amusement annuls
  it.
- Routine is what he is running from. Habit is what he blames for the world's decline, and marriage
  is condemned partly because it lands you in "a fatal continuity with custom".
- You were right that the cure is not more work and not just "looking for change". It is change of a
  specific kind: not new soil but a new method on the same soil. Limit yourself, then vary how you
  cultivate. The prisoner with the spider is the model. Your last paragraph, the small arbitrary
  moments and the accidental interest, has it right.
- "We can't change it but change our perceptions of it" is close, and the precise version is
  better: boredom can be annulled, but by amusement produced from inside, through limitation,
  remembering and forgetting, and arbitrariness, not by changing your surroundings.

### The tone
- Yes. Woman as man's ruin, a woman's love as pretence and weakness, the beauty who neither sews nor
  spins, the gypsy woman as a burden to her husband. It is A's voice, and it is meant to be heard as
  a life-view, not as Kierkegaard. Judge William's first letter in volume 2, "The Aesthetic
  Validity of Marriage", is the direct reply and is on the syllabus for November. On an exam, every
  line here is A's.

### Marriage and "the courage to break off"
- Your first sentence is A. Never enter into marriage; promising love for ever is easy and means
  nothing.
- Your second is A too, and it is the essay's one line about love rather than marriage: when two
  people fall in love and sense they are destined for each other, have the courage to break off,
  because by continuing there is everything to lose and nothing to gain, a paradox for feeling but
  not for the understanding.
- Your third, "if you truly loved them you would break it off", is not A. His reason is not the
  other person's good. It is that the erotic should have infinity, but poetic infinity, which fits
  in an hour as well as a month, and that a lasting relationship makes you "several" and costs your
  freedom. The break is self-preservation for the aesthete, not a gift to the beloved.

### Your page
- "Society making it more reasonable to deal with boredom, hence the aggravating evil": A's version
  is that the world goes backwards because boredom keeps growing, and that where Rome at least had
  bread and circuses, the present day hastens the fall with assemblies and thrift.
- "Panic and circenses" is *panem et circenses*, bread and circuses, Juvenal's phrase for what the
  Roman people were reduced to wanting.
- "Slave to our emotions" is not A's view; his advice is to have moods in your power and use them.
