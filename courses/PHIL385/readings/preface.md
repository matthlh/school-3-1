## What the Preface is doing
One idea per bullet. Read once, then let the quiz do the rest.

- **Your one idea is the right one.** The Preface opens by doubting the philosophical thesis that "the outer is the inner and the inner is the outer". That is Hegel's formula. Eremita says people carry secrets their outside never shows, so the outer does not reveal the inner.
- **The desk story acts that doubt out.** Seven years earlier Eremita saw an old writing desk (a secretaire) at a second-hand dealer's. He offered too little, was refused, and eventually bought it anyway. Before a trip to the country he needed money from a drawer that stuck. He hit the desk with a hatchet. The blow sprang open a hidden compartment full of papers. A desk he knew from the outside had an inside he never suspected.
- **The papers are two sets by two unknown men.** A's papers are aesthetic essays and aphorisms, on fine vellum paper in quarto. B's papers are letters to A, ethical in tone, on foolscap in a business-like hand. Eremita does not know their names, so he calls them A and B. From the letters he learns that B is a court assessor (a judge) named Wilhelm, and married. That is why B is known as Judge William. A stays anonymous.
- **Nested authors.** The last of A's papers is "The Seducer's Diary". A claims he only found it and copied it. Eremita does not believe him and suspects A wrote it. He compares the layers to a Chinese puzzle box: one author enclosed inside another. Count the layers: Kierkegaard, then Victor Eremita, then A, then Johannes the Seducer.
- **Ordering.** A's papers had no order, so Eremita arranged them himself and put the aphorisms first, under the title Diapsalmata with the motto "ad se ipsum" (to himself), as a preliminary to the longer essays. B's letters were already in order. Volume 1 is A, volume 2 is B. Volume 2 ends with a sermon by a Jutland pastor, a friend of B, which B forwarded to A.
- **Why "Either/Or".** Eremita toys with the idea that A and B could be one man who lived through both views, then drops it. The title says the two views of life are simply set against each other. The book gives no verdict. When you finish it, A and B are forgotten and only the two views of life stand facing each other. The reader has to choose.
- **Signature.** The title page says "edited by Victor Eremita" (Latin: the victorious hermit). The Preface itself is signed "The Editor", Copenhagen, November 1842. The book came out in February 1843.
- **What it is prefacing, in one line.** It sets up the fiction that makes the whole book work: found papers, unknown authors, an editor who takes no side, and a reader left to decide. That is the move Kraal described in lecture 1: a pseudonym presents a perspective without endorsing it. The inner/outer doubt is the same point about people: you cannot read a person's view of life off their surface.
