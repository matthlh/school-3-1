## The desk and the hidden papers

- Victor Eremita opens by questioning Hegel's thesis that "the outer is the inner and the inner is the outer". He argues that people can keep secrets their outward appearance never reveals.
- Seven years earlier, Eremita saw an old writing desk called a secretaire at a second-hand dealer's. The dealer refused his first offer, and Eremita eventually bought the desk.
- Before leaving for the country, Eremita needed money from a drawer that had stuck. He struck the desk with a hatchet and opened a hidden compartment full of papers.
- The compartment illustrates the Preface's opening claim. Eremita knew what the desk looked like and had no idea what was inside it.

## The papers and their authors

### A and B

- A's papers are essays and aphorisms about the aesthetic view of life. They are written on fine vellum in quarto with a wide margin.
- B's papers are letters to A with an ethical tone. They are written in split columns on full sheets of beehive paper, in the style of legal documents.
- The papers give Eremita no information about the first author, so he calls him A.
- The letters name the second author as Wilhelm, an assessor in an unnamed court. This is why he is known as Judge William. Eremita calls him B so both authors have editorial names.

### The diary within A's papers

- A's last paper is "The Seducer's Diary". A claims he found it and copied it, and Eremita suspects A wrote it.
- Eremita compares the authorship to a Chinese puzzle box. The four layers are Kierkegaard, Victor Eremita, A and Johannes the Seducer, in that order.

## Eremita's editorial decisions

### The order of the two volumes

- Eremita kept A's essays in the order he found them. He left that order to chance because he could tell neither whether it was chronological nor whether it was meaningful.
- Eremita put A's loose aphorisms before the longer essays. The title *Diapsalmata* and the motto "ad se ipsum", meaning to himself, came from the slips. Eremita applied them to the whole group.
- B's letters were already in order. Eremita gave them titles because letters ordinarily have none.
- Volume 1 contains A's papers. Volume 2 contains B's papers and ends with a sermon that B forwarded to A from a friend who was a pastor in Jutland.

### The title and the reader's choice

- The title *Either/Or* came from Eremita's speculation that one man could have written both sets of papers after going through both movements. He calls that thought unhistorical and improbable, yet keeps it.
- Eremita leaves the reader to judge the two views of life. By the end, A and B recede and the views themselves remain, with no final decision embodied in any particular person.
- In lecture 1, Kraal explained that a pseudonym presents a point of view without endorsing it. The Preface makes that possible by presenting the views as found papers with separate authors and an editor who takes no side.

### The signature and publication

- The title page says "edited by Victor Eremita". The name is Latin for the victorious hermit.
- The Preface is signed "The Editor", Copenhagen, November 1842. *Either/Or* came out in February 1843.
