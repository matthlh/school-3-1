# PHIL 385 — Summary: *Either/Or*, "The Seducer's Diary"

Page numbers are your PDF's. The diary is on PDF pp. 199–331, with A's preface on pp. 199–208 and the entries beginning on p. 209. The [lectures 13–15 page](../lectures/13-17-seducers-diary-and-diapsalmata.md) covers the class discussion.

## Who wrote and edited the diary

- "The Seducer's Diary" is piece 7 in Part I of *Either/Or*. It was on Exam 3 and returns on the cumulative final.
- It opens with an epigraph from the opera *Don Giovanni*, saying his ruling passion is the young beginner.
- In the story, Johannes wrote the diary as a private record of how he seduced a girl named Cordelia. He did not mean it to be published.
- An unnamed editor, whom the book calls A, found the diary in Johannes's writing desk. Johannes had left the desk open for once, with a drawer pulled out.
- The diary was a bound quarto labelled *Commentarius perpetuus No. 4*. A copied it in a hurry because he was afraid of being caught.
- A knew Cordelia. Her first name is real, but the surname Wahl is made up.
- Cordelia gave A a bundle of Johannes's letters to her. The letters have no dates, so A puts each one where the diary gives the reason for it.
- A's preface also prints three letters Cordelia wrote to Johannes after he left her. Johannes sent them back unopened.

## The story in order

### Johannes identifies Cordelia

1. **April 4th to May 6th (PDF p. 209).** Johannes first watches other girls: one getting out of a coach, one walking on Østergade, and one waiting for her lover at an exhibition. On April 9th on Langelinie, a girl in a green cape overwhelms him and he loses sight of her. He searches for her for weeks.
2. **The 12th to the 16th (PDF p. 221).** Johannes lets a girl sheltering from the rain go because of the green cape. On the 15th he sees the girl in the green cape again by the lake between Nørreport and Østerport. He watches her from the window of a family he knows. As he rushes out, he knocks the arm of a lady serving tea. The host makes him stay for tea, and he loses her again.
3. **The 19th to the 22nd (PDF p. 226).** Johannes hears a friend call the girl Cordelia. From the Jansen family he learns that she is Cordelia Wahl, the orphaned daughter of a harsh Navy captain, and that she lives quietly with her aunt. On the 22nd he is introduced to her at Mrs Jansen's and stays cool.
4. **The 30th to June 3rd (PDF p. 230).** Johannes keeps crossing Cordelia's path without engaging her. He overhears her secretly playing a Swedish melody on the piano. He decides she has never been in love and that her ideal is Joan of Arc.

### Johannes uses Edvard to gain access

5. **June 5th to 7th (PDF p. 235).** Johannes finds Edvard, a shy merchant's son who is in love with Cordelia. He befriends Edvard and uses him to get into the Wahl home. At tea he talks farming with the aunt while Edvard courts Cordelia, and he hears every word they say.
6. **July 3rd (PDF p. 247).** Johannes builds Edvard up so that Edvard's failure will teach Cordelia to look down on ordinary love. She now hates and fears Johannes and wants to compete with him. In an undated entry soon after, he reads Bürger's *Lenore* to her from a book he had Edvard lend her. Then he kills the mood by talking about bookbinding with the aunt.
7. **July 23rd (PDF p. 253).** Having chosen an engagement as his tool, Johannes spreads a rumour that he is in love with a young girl. With Cordelia listening, he gives the aunt a deliberately ambiguous speech about the rumour and brushes off every question.

### The engagement and the turn

8. **August 2nd (PDF p. 256).** Johannes finds Cordelia alone and proposes in plain, conventional words. She sends him to her aunt, the aunt agrees, and they are engaged.
9. **August onward (PDF p. 260).** Edvard is furious but still depends on Johannes. Johannes takes Cordelia to his uncle's house, which is full of kissing engaged couples, so that she will come to dislike being engaged. He starts the **first war**, in which he retreats so that she will pursue him.
10. **The letters (no dates, PDF p. 267).** Thirty of Johannes's letters to Cordelia are printed. They grow shorter and more erotic. Once he hides a note in her knitting at a party.
11. **The turn (no date, PDF p. 309).** Johannes writes *Jacta est alea*, meaning the die is cast, and pulls away, so Cordelia uses erotic means to win him back. In a game of quoits he hints at exchanging rings, and she throws both rings straight up so he cannot catch them.
12. **September 16th (PDF p. 323).** Cordelia breaks the engagement herself. Her aunt is surprised but does not pressure her.

### The country house and the end

13. **After the break (PDF p. 329).** Cordelia goes to the country with her aunt's permission. Johannes writes to her and prepares a house for her. It has a copy of her room at home, a piano like the Jansens', and Apuleius's *Cupid and Psyche* with a sprig of myrtle marking the page.
14. **September 24th and 25th (PDF p. 330).** At a quarter to twelve at night, Johannes drives out to Cordelia. The next day he writes that it is over. He also writes that he never wants to see her again and will not say goodbye. He wonders whether he could make her believe she was the one who grew tired of him.

## Johannes's method and ideas

### How he controls the encounters

- Johannes watches without being seen: from under a street-lamp, in a shop mirror and from a window.
- Once he changes clothes in a side street where his servant is waiting, so he can meet Cordelia again unrecognised.
- He gathers information indirectly, from the Jansens' gossip, and he never has a confidant.
- He holds back on purpose. He wastes chance meetings and only grazes the edge of her life, because he believes restraint is the main condition of enjoyment.
- He uses other people as tools.
    - Edvard is his foil and his way into the house.
    - He wins over the aunt with farm talk he has studied.
- He uses the engagement as a tool. He proposes in dull, set phrases, then makes engagement look ridiculous so that Cordelia breaks it off and he takes no blame.
- He calls his method the **double movement**. His letters stir Cordelia up while his ironic conversation cools her down.
- He never asks whether she has read a letter.
- He controls what Cordelia reads: first Bürger's *Lenore*, then myths and fairy tales, and at the end *Cupid and Psyche*.
- A imagines that with some girls Johannes never made a declaration or a promise, so the girl had nothing to point to. With Cordelia he did propose, but the affair was tangled enough that he could even look like the one who was seduced.

### The two wars

- The **first war** is a war of liberation and only a game. In it he retreats so that Cordelia will pursue him.
- The **second war** is a war of conquest and a matter of life and death.
- He wants to possess Cordelia in her freedom, not through the engagement.

### What he seeks in love

- **The interesting** is his goal. A surprise has to grow out of something ordinary.
- Being in love is beautiful, but knowing that you are in love is interesting.
- Love exists only in freedom, so he wants nothing that is not freely given.
- Real enjoyment only begins when a girl is still free but has one thing left to do with her freedom, which is to give herself.
- Elsewhere he says the highest enjoyment is to be loved more than anything in the world.
- **Enjoyment** needs restraint and should be taken slowly.
- A adds that Johannes enjoys everything twice. First he enjoys the real situation, then the poetic rewriting of it in the diary.
- **Love is a fight.** It is fought hand to hand, or at a distance with the eyes, and love ends when the fighting stops.
- Once a girl has given herself completely, everything is over.
- He calls the aesthetic light and fleeting and the ethical hard and boring.
- He says an engagement has no ethical reality. Marriage is honourable but boring.
- He calls himself an aesthete and an eroticist, not a seducer. He says no love affair should last more than six months.
- He claims a **pact with the aesthetic**. Serving the idea keeps him strict, so he would never betray a girl outright.
- He plans a treatise on the theory of the kiss. In it, the first kiss differs in kind from every other kiss.

### His claims about women

- Woman is *being-for-another*, like Nature.
- Eve came from Adam's rib, not his brain.
- A woman reaches the infinite through imagination, not through thought.
- The Danish word for proposing also means *to free*.

## A's judgment and Cordelia's response

- A says Johannes tried to live poetically and could not keep poetry and reality apart.
- A says the diary is written in the **subjunctive**. It tells events as if they were happening now, not as an exact record.
- A says Johannes's punishment is purely aesthetic. Even calling it conscience is too ethical a word, because in Johannes conscience is only a higher awareness felt as restless unease.
- A says Johannes is not mad.
- A compares Johannes to a tree shedding leaves, and to a fox whose every exit from its den turns out to be another way in.
- After the affair, Cordelia goes in a circle. She forgives him, blames herself for breaking the engagement, decides he planted that plan in her, hates him, then blames herself for hating him.
- In her first letter, Cordelia calls Johannes hers and herself his, meaning it as a curse.
- Her second letter is a parable of a rich man who takes a poor girl's only lamb.
- In her third letter, she begs forgiveness for still loving him.

## Names to know

### People

- **Johannes** is the diarist and the seducer.
- **Cordelia Wahl** is the seventeen-year-old he seduces. Her name recalls Cordelia, King Lear's silent third daughter.
- **The editor**, whom the book calls A, found and copied the diary and wrote the preface.
- **Captain Wahl** was Cordelia's father, a harsh Navy captain. He and Cordelia's mother are both dead.
- **Cordelia's aunt** is her father's sister. She forbids the piano and is won over by Johannes's farm talk.
- **Edvard** is the son of the merchant Baxter and the shy suitor Johannes uses as a foil.
- **Mrs Jansen** is a widow whose daughters take cookery lessons with Cordelia at the Royal Kitchens.
- **Johannes's uncle** has the house full of engaged couples.
- **Johan** is Johannes's servant. He stages the scene at the country house.
- **Charlotte Hahn** is a girl in church whose name Johannes reads on her handkerchief. He wants only her greeting.
- **Hansine Jespersen** is a merchant's daughter riding on a peat wagon. Johannes gets her driver drunk, then offers her his carriage as if he were rescuing her.
- **Marie** is a servant girl he picks up in Frederiksberg Gardens by promising to have the banns read.
- **Licentiate Hansen** chose a very young fiancée so that he could mould her.
- **Emilie** is an earlier girl Johannes remembers. She was charming but mattered less than Cordelia.

### Works and figures cited

- **Don Giovanni** is the opera the epigraph comes from.
- **Bürger's *Lenore*** is the poem he reads aloud to Cordelia.
- **Plato's *Phaedrus*** is what he reads before the physical stage of the seduction.
- **Apuleius's *Cupid and Psyche*** is the book he leaves in the country house.
- **Ariadne and Theseus** are in an old picture he redraws so that Theseus's own love punishes him.
- **Pyramus and Thisbe** stand for the engagement as a wall between lovers.
- **Alpheus and Arethusa** are a river and a fountain that unite under the sea, in a late letter.
- **Joan of Arc** is Cordelia's ideal.
- **Diana** is his ideal of virginity.
- **Bluebeard and Don Juan** are his examples of cruelty that is not abstract.
- **Claudius's President Lars** is in the engraving he sends Cordelia after the break.
- **Cuvier** is the naturalist from whom he learned to read small clues.
- **Goethe** probably wrote the German verse Cordelia used to recite about Johannes.

### Places

- **Langelinie** is where he first sees the green cape.
- **The lake between Nørreport and Østerport** is where he sees her the second time.
- **The Strand** is where the Jansens' house is.
- **The Royal Kitchens** is where Cordelia takes cookery lessons.
- **Frederiksberg Gardens** is where servant girls walk on Sundays.
- **Crib's Forest near Nøddebo** is where he overhears a pair of lovers swearing love under a beech tree.
