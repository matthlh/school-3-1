# PHIL 385 — Summary: *Either/Or*, "The Seducer's Diary"

The diary is on PDF pp. 199–331, with A's preface on pp. 199–208 and the entries beginning on p. 209. The [lectures 13–15 page](../lectures/13-17-seducers-diary-and-diapsalmata.md) covers the class discussion.

## Who wrote and edited the diary

- "The Seducer's Diary" is piece 7 in Part I of *Either/Or*. It was on Exam 3 and returns on the cumulative final.
- The epigraph comes from *Don Giovanni* and names the young beginner as his ruling passion.
- In the story, Johannes writes the diary as a private record of his seduction of Cordelia. He intends to keep it private.
- The unnamed editor is A. He found the bound quarto in a drawer of Johannes's writing desk, which Johannes had left open. It was labelled *Commentarius perpetuus No. 4*. A copied it quickly because he was afraid of being caught.
- A knew Cordelia. He says her first name is real and that Wahl is a made-up surname.
- Cordelia gave A a bundle of Johannes's undated letters to her. A places each letter where the diary explains why Johannes wrote it.
- A prints three letters that Cordelia wrote after Johannes left her. Johannes sent them back unopened.

## How the seduction unfolds

### Johannes identifies Cordelia

- **April 4th to May 6th (PDF p. 209).** Johannes watches a girl getting out of a coach, another walking on Østergade, and another waiting for her lover at an exhibition. On April 9th at Langelinie, a girl in a green cape overwhelms him. He loses sight of her and spends weeks looking for her.
- **The 12th to the 16th (PDF p. 221).** Thinking about the girl in the green cape, Johannes lets another girl sheltering from the rain go. On the 15th he sees the girl in the cape again at the lake between Nørreport and Østerport. He watches from a family's window. As he rushes out, he knocks the arm of a lady serving tea. The host makes him stay and he loses the girl again.
- **The 19th to the 22nd (PDF p. 226).** A friend calls the girl Cordelia. The Jansen family tells Johannes that Cordelia Wahl is the orphaned daughter of a harsh Navy captain and lives quietly with her aunt. On the 22nd he meets Cordelia at Mrs Jansen's and keeps his composure.
- **The 30th to June 3rd (PDF p. 230).** Johannes crosses Cordelia's path without engaging her. He overhears her secretly playing a Swedish melody. He decides she has never been in love and that Joan of Arc is her ideal.

### Johannes uses Edvard to gain access

- **June 5th to 7th (PDF p. 235).** Johannes befriends Edvard, a shy merchant's son who loves Cordelia, to enter the Wahl home. At tea he talks to the aunt about farming while listening to everything Edvard and Cordelia say.
- **July 3rd (PDF p. 247).** Johannes encourages Edvard so that his failure will teach Cordelia to look down on ordinary love. She hates and fears Johannes and wants to compete with him.
- **Soon after July 3rd (undated).** Johannes reads Bürger's *Lenore* aloud from a book he had Edvard lend Cordelia. He breaks the mood by talking about bookbinding with her aunt.
- **July 23rd (PDF p. 253).** Johannes plans to use an engagement. He spreads a rumour that he loves a young girl and gives the aunt a deliberately ambiguous speech about it with Cordelia listening. He avoids answering her questions.

### The engagement and the two wars

- **August 2nd (PDF p. 256).** Johannes finds Cordelia alone and proposes in plain, conventional words. Cordelia sends him to her aunt, who agrees to the engagement.
- **August onward (PDF p. 260).** Edvard is furious and still depends on Johannes. Johannes takes Cordelia to his uncle's house, where engaged couples are kissing, so she will come to dislike the engagement.
- Johannes calls the **first war** a war of liberation and treats it as a game. He retreats so that Cordelia will pursue him.
- The **second war** is a war of conquest, which he calls a matter of life and death. He wants Cordelia to give herself freely, independent of the engagement.
- **The letters (undated, PDF p. 267).** The diary prints thirty of Johannes's letters. They grow shorter and more erotic. He once hides a note in Cordelia's knitting at a party.
- **The turn (undated, PDF p. 309).** Johannes writes *Jacta est alea*, meaning the die is cast. He pulls away and Cordelia uses erotic means to win him back. During quoits he suggests exchanging rings. She throws both rings straight up so he cannot catch them.
- **September 16th (PDF p. 323).** Cordelia breaks the engagement herself. Her aunt is surprised and lets her decide.

### The country house and the end

- **After the break (PDF p. 329).** Cordelia goes to the country with her aunt's permission. Johannes writes to her and prepares a house with a copy of her room, a piano like the Jansens' and Apuleius's *Cupid and Psyche*. A sprig of myrtle marks the page.
- **September 24th and 25th (PDF p. 330).** At a quarter to twelve at night, Johannes drives out to see Cordelia. The next day he writes that the affair is over. He wants neither to see her again nor to say goodbye. He wonders whether he could convince her she was the one who got tired of him.

## Johannes's method and ideas

### How he controls the encounters

- Johannes watches without being seen from under a street-lamp, in a shop mirror and from a window. Once he changes clothes in a side street where his servant is waiting so he can meet Cordelia again unrecognised.
- He learns about her from the Jansens' gossip and never tells a confidant. Edvard gives Johannes a way into the home and serves as a foil. Johannes studies farming to win over the aunt.
- He passes up chance meetings and limits his contact with Cordelia because he considers restraint the main condition of enjoyment.
- He calls his method the **double movement**. His letters stir Cordelia up and his ironic conversation cools her down. He never asks if she has read a letter.
- He controls what Cordelia reads, starting with Bürger's *Lenore*, then myths and fairy tales, and ending with *Cupid and Psyche*.
- He makes the engagement look ridiculous so Cordelia will break it off and he can avoid blame. A imagines that with other girls, Johannes gave them no declaration or promise to point to. With Cordelia he does propose, yet the affair is complicated enough to make him look like the one seduced.

### What he seeks in love

- Johannes seeks **the interesting**. He thinks a surprise has to come out of something ordinary. Being in love is beautiful to him, and knowing you are in love is interesting.
- Johannes thinks love exists only in freedom. He wants Cordelia to give herself when she is still free and sees giving herself as the one thing left to do with her freedom. Elsewhere he says the highest enjoyment is to be loved more than anything in the world.
- Johannes thinks enjoyment needs restraint and takes time. A says Johannes enjoys each situation twice, once while it happens and again when he rewrites it poetically.
- Johannes describes love as a fight, whether hand to hand or from a distance through glances. The affair ends when the fighting stops and the girl has given herself completely.
- He calls the aesthetic light and fleeting and the ethical hard and boring. He says an engagement has no ethical reality, and he calls marriage honourable and boring.
- He calls himself an aesthete and an eroticist and rejects the name seducer. He says no affair should last more than six months.
- He claims a **pact with the aesthetic**. He says serving the idea keeps him strict and stops him from betraying a girl outright.
- He plans a treatise on the kiss in which the first kiss differs in kind from every other kiss.

### His claims about women

- Johannes describes woman as *being-for-another*, like Nature.
- He points to Eve coming from Adam's rib instead of his brain.
- He says women reach the infinite through imagination and treats thought as a different route.
- He points out that the Danish word for proposing also means *to free*.

## A's judgment and Cordelia's response

- A says Johannes tried to live poetically and lost the distinction between poetry and reality.
- The diary uses the **subjunctive** in A's account. It recreates events as if they were happening now instead of giving an exact record.
- A calls Johannes's punishment aesthetic. He thinks conscience is too ethical a word for what Johannes feels, which is a higher awareness experienced as restless unease. A says Johannes is sane.
- A compares Johannes to a tree shedding leaves and to a fox whose every exit from its den is another way in.
- After the affair, Cordelia forgives Johannes and blames herself for breaking the engagement. She then suspects he planted the idea in her, hates him and blames herself for hating him.
- In her first letter, Cordelia calls Johannes hers and herself his, meaning it as a curse.
- Her second letter is a parable about a rich man who takes a poor girl's only lamb.
- In her third letter, she begs forgiveness for still loving Johannes.

## People, places and references

### People in the diary

- **Johannes** is the diarist and the seducer.
- **Cordelia Wahl** is seventeen. Her name recalls King Lear's silent third daughter.
- **Captain Wahl** was Cordelia's father and a harsh Navy captain. Both of Cordelia's parents are dead.
- **Cordelia's aunt** is Captain Wahl's sister. She forbids Cordelia from playing the piano and takes to Johannes because of his farm talk.
- **Edvard** is the son of the merchant Baxter.
- **Mrs Jansen** is a widow. Her daughters and Cordelia take cookery lessons at the Royal Kitchens.
- **Johan** is Johannes's servant. He stages the scene at the country house.
- **Charlotte Hahn** is a girl whose name Johannes reads on a handkerchief at church. He wants only her greeting.
- **Hansine Jespersen** is a merchant's daughter riding on a peat wagon. Johannes gets her driver drunk and offers her his carriage as if rescuing her.
- **Marie** is a servant girl Johannes approaches in Frederiksberg Gardens. He promises to have the banns read.
- **Licentiate Hansen** chose a very young fiancée so he could mould her.
- **Emilie** was charming but mattered less to Johannes than Cordelia.

### Works and figures

- Johannes reads **Plato's *Phaedrus*** before the physical stage of the seduction.
- He redraws an old picture of **Ariadne and Theseus** so Theseus's own love punishes him.
- **Pyramus and Thisbe** represent the engagement as a wall between the lovers.
- **Alpheus and Arethusa** are a river and a fountain that unite under the sea in a late letter.
- **Diana** is his ideal of virginity.
- **Bluebeard and Don Juan** are his examples of cruelty in action.
- **Claudius's President Lars** appears in the engraving he sends Cordelia after the break.
- Johannes learned to read small clues from **Cuvier**, the naturalist.
- **Goethe** probably wrote the German verse Cordelia used to recite about Johannes.

### Places in the story

- The Jansens live on **the Strand**.
- Servant girls walk in **Frederiksberg Gardens** on Sundays.
- In **Crib's Forest near Nøddebo**, Johannes overhears a couple swearing their love under a beech tree.
