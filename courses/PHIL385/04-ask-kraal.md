## Ready to ask (from lec 2, Mon Sep 14)

- **The one to lead with.** You said A's and B's views are not set in stone, and that which one
  applies can depend on the situation. But in "Equilibrium" the Judge seems to attack exactly that,
  treating a both/and as the evasion. Is the context-dependence at the level of the reader, so the
  book meets different people differently, or at the level of a life, so one person can rightly
  live aesthetically in one domain and ethically in another?
  - What to listen for: whether he separates the choice *between* the views from the choice *to
    choose at all*. That distinction is what the Judge's argument turns on.
- **Does the ethical replace the aesthetic or contain it?** "The Aesthetic Validity of Marriage"
  sounds like the second, since the Judge argues the married life is the more beautiful one.
  "Equilibrium" sounds like a hard either/or. Which is his considered position?
- **Is Judge William the answer, or just the next view?** B is a pseudonym too. Later in the
  authorship, Climacus treats the ethical as not the final word. Should we read B as answering A,
  or as a standpoint that gets superseded in turn?
- **Is A named Johannes?** Eremita says the papers give no information at all about the first
  author, and Johannes is named only inside the diary, in Cordelia's letters. Is "A is Johannes"
  right, or is it right only if we accept Eremita's suspicion that A wrote the diary himself?
- **Where does the religious view enter?** The book ends with a sermon by a Jutland pastor whom
  neither A nor B wrote. Is a religious standpoint already present inside *Either/Or*, or does it
  only arrive later in the authorship?
- **Is the subtitle aimed at Hegel?** The book is *Either/Or: A Fragment of Life*. Is "fragment"
  a jab at the completeness a system claims, or is it just describing found papers?
- **Choice without a guarantee.** Kierkegaard's individual has to choose without anything
  underwriting the choice. If some system could reliably tell you what to choose, would that remove
  the choice or only disguise it?
  - This is the defensible version of the AI question. It asks about Kierkegaard rather than about
    technology, so it is answerable in a philosophy office hour.
