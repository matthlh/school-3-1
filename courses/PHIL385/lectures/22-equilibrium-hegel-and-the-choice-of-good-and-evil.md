# PHIL 385 — Lec 22 (Mon Nov 16) — Why good and evil are a matter of will

## Thought, freedom and historical explanation

- B gives **logic, nature and history** to thought. **Mediation** means making sense of opposing things by showing how each belongs to a wider whole. A historian can explain how different decisions helped produce a single outcome.
- B takes historical explanation to be **relative mediation**. Individuals decide freely, and their actions then enter a larger order of events. That order is still developing. Looking back can explain what a choice became, while the person faced with it had to decide what to do.
- The tree on the board used B's list of the spheres of thought. Hegel's own tree has **Logic, Nature and Spirit**, with world history under Spirit. His **ethical life** has the family, civil society and the state. His example of marriage as an ethical tie helps explain the background to B's first letter. [Lecture 21 and Kraal's steps](21-equilibrium-hegels-framework-and-the-real-either-or.md)

## What B is saying on Hannay p. 524

1. Thought can tell a person how two things differ. For example, it can compare two possible actions by likely outcome. B calls these **relative differences** because the answer depends on what is being compared and on the standard used.
2. Good and evil are an **absolute difference** in B's account. When someone freely chooses themselves and takes responsibility for how they live, the distinction comes into force for them.
3. That is what B means by saying that the good exists by virtue of his willing it. A person enters a relation to good and evil through the will. B says the categories themselves have **absolute validity**, so an individual's preference cannot turn evil into good.

- Your notes ask whether the view is objective or relative. B claims absolute validity for the categories and denies that philosophy can demonstrate them as facts. His point is about how anyone comes to stand in the ethical and make a choice there.
- B also distinguishes **positive freedom** from *liberum arbitrium*, bare freedom to pick either option. Positive freedom is realized by excluding evil. An explanation in thought can relate both sides to a whole. The person exercising freedom acts on a choice and excludes the other course. [Reading, PDF pp. 424–427 and 456–457](../readings/equilibrium.md)

## What choosing has to do with love and God

- On Hannay p. 480 and following (PDF pp. 423–424), B's first either/or asks whether the person will live in terms of good and evil. Once they take that step, they face the **absolute either/or** between good and evil in their actions. The aesthetic factor is how a person immediately is. The ethical factor is how the person becomes who they become.
- Love is central to the first letter because B asks how the marriage vow carries love into the future. In the second letter the focus is the person who makes such choices. B describes that person accepting themselves before an eternal power (PDF p. 427).
- The love that matters in this part of the second letter is the love of God. B says it is expressed through **repentance**. In choosing oneself, a person also faces the guilt and history that belong to their actual life (PDF p. 451). That is how choice, selfhood and love of God meet here.
