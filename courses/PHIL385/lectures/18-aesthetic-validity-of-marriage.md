# PHIL 385 — Lec 18 (Mon Nov 2) — Why B thinks marriage can keep love alive

## What B is trying to show

- A thinks first love is exciting and marriage makes it ordinary. B, the married Judge William, takes up that challenge in his first letter to A in Part II of *Either/Or*.
- B has two tasks. First, show that marriage can be **aesthetic**, meaning beautiful or poetic. Second, show how the couple can keep that beauty through time and hardship.
- B writes as someone who cares for A like a son, a brother and a friend. He thinks A keeps finding exciting beginnings and moves on before they become anything. He warns A about what he is doing to himself, and he warns the world about A.

## Why duty helps love last

1. A lasting relationship eventually asks something of the people in it. Think of having to be patient, honest or dependable when the feeling is less intense.
2. If love and duty were enemies, love would have to defeat those demands to last. If it could not, A's own picture of love would leave it unable to survive real life.
3. B says duty can help love survive. Once the couple has freely promised to love each other, those demands give them specific ways to keep the promise. For B, the duty is **truly to love**.

- That is Kraal's argument in lecture. The shared notes use six months as an example of when responsibilities show up. B's point in the letter is that they arise sooner or later, in romantic love as well as marriage. The duty passage is around Hannay pp. 468–469 (PDF pp. 408–409).
- B calls the **aesthetic, the ethical and the religious** the three great allies. The couple begins in love, undertakes a duty through the vow, and receives its love as a gift from God.

## What the vow changes

- The Church assumes that the couple is in love and asks for a **vow**. Kraal dates the Christian marriage vow to around 1100. For B, the vow expresses their intention to keep loving through time.
- Suppose two people are in love and a hardship comes up. Their promise gives them a reason to face it together. How they work through it becomes part of the love they share. B calls that growth their **inner history** and the direction given by the vow the **law of motion**.
- B says love has the **stamp of the eternal**. Lust wants satisfaction in the instant. The initial feeling of love carries the sense that it should last, and the will can carry this intention into the future.
- Your comparison to motivation and determination helps here. The first feeling can come on its own. Continuing to love involves what the couple decides to do with that feeling. B calls the patience it takes to stay with someone **possession** and the excitement of winning them **conquest**.
