# PHIL 385 — Lec 10 (Wed Oct 7) — missed: the first two stages, from the reading

The essay’s first two stages cover the gap between the [lecture 9](09-the-musical-erotic.md) discussion of Cherubino and Friday’s discussion of Don Giovanni. They follow the essay’s discussion of music and language (pp. 77–83). The [question bank](../02-questions.md) has a “Lec 10” section.

## From the reading

### First stage. Desire dreams.

- A uses Cherubino, "the Page" from *The Marriage of Figaro*, to show desire before it knows what it wants.
- Desire and its object have yet to separate. The object exists only in the dream of desire itself, so desire is a longing without an aim.
- Cherubino’s mood is quiet and melancholy. In the aria Kraal played on Monday, he searches for "a good outside myself" without knowing what it is or who holds it.

### Second stage. Desire seeks.

- Desire wakes and turns outward. It sees many possible objects, which A pictures as all the women in the world.
- Desire looks for someone it can desire. It has yet to single anyone out (p. 90).
- The mood is cheerful and lively. A’s example is Papageno, the bird-catcher with the panpipe in *The Magic Flute*. Papageno wants "a net for girls" to catch them by the dozen.
- Papageno represents the stage even though Tamino is the opera’s hero.

### Third stage. Desire finds a particular object.

- Don Giovanni desires each particular woman as an instance of woman in general.
- This stage keeps the dreamer’s pull toward an ideal and the seeker’s turn toward many possible objects.
- A orders the stages by the development of desire. The operas came out in a different order.
