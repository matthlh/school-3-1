## From the reading

### Where these pages sit

- The two stages come after the essay’s discussion of music and language (pp. 77–83) and before the third stage. Page 90 is in the second stage.
- For each of the first two stages, A takes one character as its picture, not the whole opera.

### First stage. Dreaming.

- A uses Cherubino, "the Page" from *The Marriage of Figaro*, to show desire before it knows what it wants.
- Desire has not yet woken up.
- Desire and its object have yet to separate. The object exists only in the dream of desire itself, so desire is a longing without an aim.
- Desire at this stage is in love with love itself.
- The mood is quiet and melancholy, not joyful.
- In the aria Kraal played on Monday, Cherubino searches for "a good outside myself" without knowing what it is or who holds it.

### Second stage. Seeking.

- Desire wakes up. Its object separates from it and appears outside it.
- The object appears as many, which A pictures as all the women in the world. Desire turns outward and goes looking among them.
- Desire looks for one it can desire. It has yet to desire any one of them (p. 90).
- The mood is cheerful and lively, the opposite of the first stage’s melancholy.
- A’s example is Papageno, the bird-catcher with the panpipe in *The Magic Flute*. Papageno’s aria wants "a net for girls" to catch them by the dozen.
- A takes Papageno as the picture of this stage, not Tamino, the opera’s hero.

### Third stage. Desiring.

- Dreaming has no object. Seeking has many objects but wants none in particular.
- Desiring is Don Giovanni. He desires each particular woman as an instance of woman in general.
- This stage keeps the dreamer’s pull toward an ideal and the seeker’s turn toward the many.
- A orders the stages by the growth of desire, not by the dates of the operas.

- The "a net for girls" line is from "Der Vogelfänger bin ich ja".
- Kraal may have played Papageno’s other aria, "Ein Mädchen oder Weibchen" (Act 2), instead.
- What Kraal said about Tamino or about *The Magic Flute* as a whole is unknown.
