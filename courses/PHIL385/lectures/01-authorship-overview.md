## Clarifications
- **Hegel:** died 14 Nov 1831 (Berlin, cholera). His followers split into **Right (Old) Hegelians**, who read the system as compatible with orthodox Christianity (personal God, immortality), and **Left (Young) Hegelians** (Strauss, Feuerbach, Bauer, later Marx), who read it as incompatible with theism. Strauss himself coined the right/centre/left labels (1837), borrowed from seating in the French parliament. Hegel presented his system as the conceptual form of Christian truth and said he was and would remain a Lutheran.
- **"Straus book":** David Friedrich **Strauss**, *The Life of Jesus, Critically Examined* (*Das Leben Jesu*, 1835–36). It reads the Gospel narratives as **myth** produced by the early Christian community rather than history — the book that made the theological dispute explode.
- **Pantheism = atheism:** since the Pantheism Controversy (Jacobi vs Mendelssohn, 1785, over Lessing's Spinozism), pantheism (God = the whole, no personal God) counted in Germany as atheism in disguise. Hegel's critics: his Absolute Spirit is impersonal → pantheism → atheism, whatever he said about Lutheranism.
- **"___ tasked by king of Prussia":** **F. W. J. Schelling**, called to Berlin in 1841 by **Friedrich Wilhelm IV** to "stamp out the dragon-seed of Hegelian pantheism" (the king's phrase). Kierkegaard left Copenhagen in Oct 1841 to hear these lectures (winter 1841–42, "Philosophy of Revelation"; Engels, Bakunin and Burckhardt were in the same audience), was enthusiastic at first, then bored and disappointed, and was back in Copenhagen by March 1842. The Berlin trip came right after he broke his engagement to Regine Olsen (Oct 1841).
- **Dissertation:** *On the Concept of Irony with Continual Reference to Socrates*, defended 29 Sept 1841 (magister degree, equivalent to a doctorate).
- **1843 debut:** *Either/Or* (Danish *Enten–Eller*), published 20 Feb 1843, "edited by **Victor Eremita**" — papers of "A" (the aesthete, vol. 1) and "B" (Judge William, vol. 2). Kraal's "public debut" = debut of the pseudonymous authorship; the dissertation (1841) and a review (1838) were earlier, under his own name.
- **"He publishes 80 books"** — almost certainly misheard. The count Kraal is working with is **8 pseudonymous books + 18 discourses (1843–46)**. In 1843 alone he published six (Either/Or, Fear and Trembling, Repetition, three discourse volumes). Verify next class.

- **"Crumbs":** Hannay translates *Philosophiske Smuler* as *Philosophical Crumbs*; most editions say *Fragments*. Same book.
- **"Stages of life, but aren't they all?"** — yes: the three spheres (aesthetic → ethical → religious) run through the whole authorship (Either/Or is aesthetic vs ethical). *Stages on Life's Way* is the book that lays all three out in one volume (a banquet / a judge on marriage / "Guilty?/Not Guilty?").
- **Why Crumbs and Postscript share a pseudonym:** the Postscript is the sequel — full title *Concluding Unscientific Postscript to Philosophical Crumbs*. Both are by **Johannes Climacus**, who writes as a *non-Christian* asking how one becomes a Christian (named after John Climacus, the 7th-c. monk of *The Ladder of Divine Ascent*). The Postscript was meant to *conclude* the authorship, hence "concluding".
- **Pseudonyms ≠ his views — where he says so:** "A First and Last Explanation," appended to the Postscript (1846): he admits he wrote them but says there is "not a single word" of his own in the pseudonymous books and asks that quotations be attributed to the pseudonym, not to him. Each pseudonym is a standpoint on existence worked out from inside. This is why Kraal says he is misunderstood even by scholars: they quote a pseudonym as Kierkegaard.
- **The 18:** the *Eighteen Upbuilding Discourses* — six small volumes (2 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 3 + 4) under his own name, 1843–44, each published alongside a pseudonymous book ("with the left hand the pseudonymous works, with the right hand the discourses"). Called "upbuilding" (edifying), not sermons, because he was not ordained. They carry his own religious standpoint while the pseudonyms carry the alternatives.
- **Second authorship (1847 on):** his own, religious perspective — but not all of it is signed. *Works of Love* (1847) and *Christian Discourses* (1848) are under his own name; ***The Sickness unto Death* (1849) and *Practice in Christianity* (1850) are by the pseudonym Anti-Climacus**, with "S. Kierkegaard" as editor. Anti-Climacus is a *higher* pseudonym (the ideal Christian, above Kierkegaard) where Climacus is a lower one (below him). *The Point of View for My Work as an Author* (written 1848, published 1859) explains the whole authorship as religious from the start.
- **Love:** *Works of Love* splits **preferential love** (erotic love, friendship, love of country — your "animalistic/romantic" love; it loves the other as "another self," so it is self-love in disguise) from **Christian neighbour-love**: commanded ("you *shall* love"), non-preferential, loving the neighbour as yourself, with God as the "middle term." Kraal's line: he turns to love because it is the closest thing to the faith he wants to hold in his heart.
- **Existentialism link (your question):** the label is 20th-century (Jaspers' *Existenzphilosophie*, then Sartre); Kierkegaard is called its founder in hindsight because of the single individual against the System, "truth is subjectivity" (Postscript), existence as a choice between spheres (Either/Or), and the analyses of anxiety and despair. The course's last two weeks (Jaspers, Marcel, Heidegger, Sartre, de Beauvoir, Camus) draw that line explicitly.
