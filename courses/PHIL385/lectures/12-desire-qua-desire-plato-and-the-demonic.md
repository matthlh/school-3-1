## Clarifications

### The subtitle, and whether A "leaves out" the ending
- The opera's full title is *Il dissoluto punito, ossia il Don Giovanni*, "The Rake Punished, or Don Giovanni". Kraal's "punishment of the libertine" is a loose rendering of the same words. The words are Da Ponte's, 1787.
- The ending is Don Giovanni dragged down to hell by the statue of the Commendatore, the man he killed in the first scene.
- Kraal's claim is that A cherry-picks: the punishment does not fit his view, so he drops it. If an exam question asks what Kraal said, that is the answer.
- For accuracy, A does not skip the ending. He writes about the Commendatore's return in the section on the opera's structure, and calls it "a monstrous irony": Don Giovanni can beat every obstacle, but you cannot kill a ghost, and only a spirit can coerce him because he is immediacy and spirit is its negation. Earlier he says that once reflection enters, the sensual realm shows itself as the realm of sin, "Don Juan is slain, the music comes to an end". So A keeps the ending but reads it aesthetically, as spirit ending immediacy, not as a moral lesson about hell. See the [reading page](../readings/musical-erotic.md).

### Plato, with the standard names
- *Republic* (Book IV): the soul has three parts, reason, the spirited part (Greek *thumos*, Kraal's "drive") and appetite (desire). Justice in the soul is each part doing its own work under reason, and the just person is also the beautiful one, the Greek pairing of the good and the beautiful.
- *Phaedrus*: the soul is a charioteer driving two horses. The charioteer is reason. The white horse is the noble, spirited part, which Kraal called drive. The black horse is appetite, desire. The aim is to keep them pulling in proportion, not to kill the black horse.
- *Symposium*, the earlier note on your page: Diotima's ladder of love climbs from desire for one beautiful body, to all beautiful bodies, to beautiful souls, laws and knowledge, and at the top to the Form of Beauty itself.
- The contrast with A is the point of the digression. Plato's ladder goes upward, from the particular body to the abstract Form, and the climb is the work of reason. A's stages go the other way, from the abstract dream of "the one" to the particular woman, and every stage is immediate, with no reason in it.

### Greeks, Christianity and "a Christian art"
- The Greek view in Kraal's picture: desire is one part of a balanced soul, nothing to be ashamed of, so the Greeks "would have smiled" at Don Giovanni.
- The Christian view: desire is sinful, something to reduce. Kraal mentioned New Testament passages; examples are Matthew 5:28 on looking with lust, Galatians 5:16–17 on flesh against spirit, and 1 John 2:16 on "the lust of the flesh".
- A's own claim, which you already have from page 75 of Hannay: Christianity set spirit against flesh and so excluded the sensual, and in excluding it made the sensual a principle of its own for the first time. That is why music, the art of that principle, is a Christian art and was not developed in antiquity.
- A's version of the Greek side is slightly different from Kraal's: the Greeks did not lack desire, but their love was "of the soul", in harmony with the sensual, so they had no idea of a seducer. Hercules had many women and was still no seducer.

### Why "demonic"
- Kraal's chain: desire can overpower a person; being overpowered is what "demonic" means; music is the medium of desire; so music is demonic.
- A's text puts it this way. When the sensual is excluded by spirit but not yet judged by it, it takes the form of the demonic "in aesthetic indifference". Don Juan is the demonic specified as the sensual, and Faust is the demonic specified as the spiritual. The Venusberg, the mountain where the sensual has its kingdom, is where "language has no home".

### "Desire qua desire" and "no half and half"
- Desire qua desire means desire taken as itself, with no reflection, reasoning or plan mixed in. That is what makes Don Giovanni immediate, and why A says he is not really a seducer in the planning sense.
- "No half and half" is A's "absolutely". Sensual love is "absolutely faithless", it loves not one but all, and Don Giovanni is always "absolutely victorious", with none of the doubt that love of the soul has.
