## How the ethical can be more aesthetic

- A judges love by how beautiful and exciting it feels. Kraal reads B as saying that commitment can give those same lovers a deeper kind of beauty. They live through changes together and acquire things like trust and fidelity.
- Kraal connects this to Plato's *Republic*, Book VI. His point in class was that the beautiful gets its value from the good. Applied to the essay, the ethical supplies the conditions under which love can develop its beauty.
- B calls this the **transfiguration of first love**. The same love continues and gains a history through what the couple chooses to do.

## What Kraal's examples are doing

- **Children.** Kraal compares dying alone with dying among people you love. His example shows a possible good inside a shared life. B calls children a blessing and rejects using them as the purpose for marrying. His image is a poor woman in the square holding her baby.
- **Openness.** A imagines keeping his wife in another wing of the castle so they stay mysterious to each other. B thinks knowing a person well makes a deeper love possible. He wants **privacy with outsiders and openness between spouses**. Kraal's heading "confinement" points to the secrecy that keeps A from giving himself to another person.
- **Cordelia.** Kraal applies B's test of the **stamp of the eternal** to *The Seducer's Diary*. In the final entry, Johannes says he never wants to see Cordelia again after their night together. The affair is designed to end once he has seduced her (PDF pp. 330–331). This is Kraal's comparison across the two texts.

## The argument from love to the will

1. Marriage starts with people who are already in love. B calls being in love the **substance of marriage** (PDF pp. 347–348).
2. First love includes sensual attraction and carries a claim to last. Lust centres on what can be enjoyed right now (PDF pp. 342–343, 352).
3. A claim about forever has to be lived through actual days and years. The **intention** in the vow makes that the couple's task. This is Kraal's reason for calling marriage **promise keeping** (PDF pp. 358, 376, 408–409).

- **Conjugal** means married. Kraal's diagram shows how romantic love becomes conjugal love. For B, thanking God lifts that same love into a **higher concentricity**. The vow gives it a future the couple can work toward.
- The diagram's note about civil marriage raises a distinction. Legal recognition can happen without anyone asking whether the couple is in love. B's claim is about what makes marriage meaningful. In his account the Church already assumes the couple is in love when it asks for a vow.
- Some readers hear Hegel in B's language of **mediation** and in his view that marriage gives love an ethical form. Lecture 21 explains the comparison with Hegel's account of marriage.
