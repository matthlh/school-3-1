## The three facts that decide your grade here

**1. 45% of this course is 21 multiple-choice questions**, marked on **factual accuracy alone**.
Three exams × 7 questions. That means **each single MC question is worth ~2.1% of your final
grade.** Miss two on one exam and you've lost 4.3 points off your course total. This is the
highest-stakes-per-question course you're taking, and it's pure recall — exactly what the
question bank is for.

**2. Exams 2, 3, and 4 are not cumulative.** Exam 2 doesn't touch Exam 1's material, and so on.
Only the final is cumulative. So each in-term exam is a small, closed, fully-learnable block of
~3 weeks of lectures. There is no reason to ever go into one of these underprepared.

**3. No slides. No recordings. No instructor notes.** Section 8.3–8.5. **Your lecture notes are
the only record that exists.** If you miss a class or write a bad page, that material is gone —
you'd be asking classmates for notes. The two-column question page matters more in this course
than in any other.

## Essay exam — how it's marked
Factual accuracy, **clarity, relevance, cogency** — and explicitly **relative to your peers**:
"If one student's answers are clearer, or more cogent... the TA will seek to have this element
of relative performance reflected in the grading." Graded by TAs, not Kraal.

500 words is short. Structure beats flourish: state the position, give the argument in numbered
steps, give the strongest objection, respond. Clear and plain wins against classmates writing
impressionistic prose about angst.
