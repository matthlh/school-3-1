# What to paste me

Ranked by how much it changes what I can do for you. You do not need all of it.

## Tier 1 — needed before I'm useful (~30 min)

**1. ~~Syllabi for STAT 251, ASIA 250, and PHIL 385~~ — done (2026-09-10).**
Paste the whole thing, raw — don't clean it up. Drop it into the
`RAW SYLLABUS PASTE` block in `courses/<CODE>/00-syllabus.md`, or just paste it in chat and say
"this is STAT 251." What I actually pull out of it:
- exact grading weights → tells me where your hours are worth the most
- exam dates and **format** (MC? proofs? open book? formula sheet?) → changes how I quiz you
- late/drop policies (is the lowest quiz dropped? is there a curve?)
- the topic list → becomes the gap-check at T-10 before each exam

All four are in `courses/<CODE>/00-syllabus.md`; logistics in `03-logistics.md`.

**2. The course schedule / topic list** for each course, if it's separate from the syllabus.
This is what lets me tell you at T-10 "the syllabus covers Bayes' theorem and you have no
notes on it."

## Tier 2 — makes the quizzing sharp (ongoing, ~1 min each)

**3. Your lecture page, after each lecture.**
Photo of the physical page is fine — I'll read it. Say `log STAT251 lec 3`.
This is the main input. Everything downstream runs on it.

**4. Any past exams, practice midterms, or sample problems** the profs release.
Highest-value single item after the syllabus. Past exam questions tell me the *form* of
question each prof writes, which is most of what a mock exam needs to be right.

## Tier 3 — helpful, whenever

**5. Assignment and essay prompts**, when they're posted — especially PHIL 385 and ASIA 250.
Essay grading is where your ceiling is lowest, so lead time matters most there.
**6. Slide decks**, if you'd rather I extract questions than do it yourself.
**7. Anything the prof said is "important" or "will be on the exam."** Even one line. For the
PHIL and ASIA courses, prof emphasis is the single best predictor of the exam.
**8. Your grades as they come back.** Lets me redirect hours toward whatever is actually at risk
instead of guessing.

## What I don't need
Textbooks, full readings, or lecture recordings. Too much volume for too little signal — and
the point of this system is that you're not reprocessing bulk material anyway.
